Think of learning to build with bricks analogy

The pillars are knowing what a single brick can do. SOLID is the rules of good bricklaying — don't build so the whole wall falls when you replace one brick. Patterns are proven structures other builders named: an arch, a buttress, a staircase. LLD is being handed a plot and a brief, and actually building the house. Advanced is the high-rise with elevators and fire codes (concurrency). Drills are the timed site inspection.

Wait — why does "design" even matter?

Any working program is, by definition, "correct enough" today. Design is about tomorrow: when the requirements change (and they always do), is your code something you can safely extend — or a house of cards where touching one thing breaks three others?

Good object-oriented design is mostly about managing change: keeping related things together, keeping unrelated things apart, and hiding details so that most of the system doesn't care when one part changes. Everything in this guide is a tool for that one goal.

Hide what changes

Encapsulation & abstraction let you change how something works without anyone noticing.

Keep parts loose

SOLID & the patterns reduce coupling, so one change doesn't ripple across the whole codebase.

Share a vocabulary

"Use a Strategy here" says in two words what would take a paragraph. Patterns are a shared language.

Language & style

All examples are Python 3.13 and use modern idioms: dataclasses, abc.ABC / @abstractmethod, typing.Protocol for interfaces, enum.Enum, @property, and match/case. The design ideas themselves are language-agnostic — we note where Python differs from classic Java/C++ OOP.
