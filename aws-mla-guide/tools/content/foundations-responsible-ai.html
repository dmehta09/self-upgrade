Foundations · responsible AI

Build ML that is fair, explainable, and safe to ship.

MLA-C02 expects you to spot bias, fairness gaps, transparency, and safety constraints — not as ethics essays, but as architecture choices. SageMaker Clarify and Bedrock Guardrails are the AWS-native levers; this lesson previews where they appear later.

01 Four pillars on the exam

When a stem mentions protected attributes, explainability, harmful content, or regulatory audit, you are in responsible-AI territory. Pick the tool that matches the lifecycle phase.

Bias & fairness
Measure disparate impact across groups (e.g., age, zip code proxies). Clarify pre-training and post-training bias reports.
Transparency
Explain predictions — SHAP values for tabular models; citation-style answers for RAG.
Privacy & consent
De-identify in prep; encrypt at rest/in transit; IAM least privilege on training data.
GenAI safety
Guardrails filter topics, PII, toxicity; block jailbreak patterns on Bedrock invoke.

02 Acme Health and Ledger Bank

Responsible AI shows up as constraints in both stories — not optional polish.

Acme Health · readmission model
  • Clarify checks bias on race/sex proxies before approval
  • Clinicians need SHAP-style reason codes per prediction
  • Chatbot gets Guardrails — no diagnosis, no PHI leakage
Ledger Bank · credit & fraud
  • Fair lending: monitor approval rates by demographic segment
  • Explainability for adverse action notices (regulatory)
  • Model Monitor tracks drift that could reintroduce bias
In plain English analogy

Bias testing is like calibrating a hospital thermometer across wards — if it reads high for one group, treatment decisions skew. Guardrails are the nurse who stops the intern from saying something harmful to a patient family.

03 Clarify and Guardrails — preview pointers

You will go deeper in modeling and GenAI lessons. For MLA-C02 foundations, know when to reach for each — not every API parameter.

SageMaker Clarify
  • Pre-training bias metrics on datasets
  • Post-training bias on model predictions
  • SHAP explainability for tabular/classical ML
  • Runs in Processing jobs or training workflow
Bedrock Guardrails
  • Content filters (hate, violence, sexual)
  • Denied topics and word filters
  • PII redaction / blocking on FM output
  • Applied at Bedrock invoke — GenAI path
Common trap gotcha

Guardrails for tabular fraud explainability. Guardrails filter FM text — they don't produce SHAP charts for an XGBoost endpoint. Clarify does that. Match the tool to model type.

Acme's Bedrock chatbot must block medical diagnosis advice and redact patient names in responses. Best AWS control?

Right. GenAI content safety → Guardrails. Clarify SHAP is for classical model explainability; Model Monitor watches drift, not conversational policy.

04 Exam rapid-fire

When do you use Clarify vs Guardrails?warm-up
Model answer

Clarify for dataset/model bias and SHAP explainability on SageMaker-trained models. Guardrails for Bedrock FM safety — toxic content, denied topics, PII on generated text.

Say it out loud say it

"Clarify for bias and SHAP on SageMaker models; Guardrails for Bedrock text safety."

Ledger needs adverse-action explanations for declined loans. What service?core
Model answer

SageMaker Clarify explainability (SHAP/feature importance) on the credit model, integrated into the inference workflow — plus ongoing bias monitoring in production via Model Monitor + Clarify metrics where applicable.

Say it out loud say it

"Adverse action on a tabular credit model → Clarify SHAP, not Guardrails."

Name two responsible-AI checks before promoting a model to production.stretch
Model answer

(1) Clarify pre/post-training bias report against policy thresholds. (2) Document lineage in Model Registry + encryption/IAM review. For GenAI add Guardrails policy testing on representative prompts.

Say it out loud say it

"Bias report, lineage, and for GenAI — Guardrails smoke tests before prod."
