GenAI · Bedrock models

Pick the FM: latency, cost, capability.

Bedrock exposes multiple foundation models behind one API. MLA-C02 stems force trade-offs — smallest model that meets quality, on-demand vs provisioned throughput, and whether the model is available in your Region.

01 Model choice triangle

Read the stem for latency (real-time chat vs batch), cost (token volume), and capability (reasoning, long context, multimodal). Default to the smallest model that satisfies the hardest constraint — upgrade only when quality fails in eval.

flowchart TD
  need["User need"] --> lat{"Low latency?"}
  lat -->|yes| small["Smaller / faster FM"]
  lat -->|no| cap{"Complex reasoning?"}
  cap -->|yes| large["Larger FM"]
  cap -->|no| small
  small --> region{"In your Region?"}
  region -->|no| alt["Pick available model or Region"]
Acme Health
Nurse triage chatbot — sub-second replies, high volume → smaller fast model; escalate to larger FM only for complex case summaries offline.
Ledger Bank
Batch contract summarization overnight → larger model OK; optimize cost with batch inference, not provisioned throughput unless steady 24/7.
Multimodal
Image + text stems → pick FM that supports vision in Bedrock catalog for that Region.
Long context
Whole policy PDF in prompt vs RAG — long-context FM costs more; RAG often wins exam “cost-effective” stems.

02 On-demand vs provisioned throughput

On-demand — pay per token, no commitment; best for spiky or POC traffic. Provisioned throughput — reserved model capacity for predictable high QPS and latency SLAs; exam when stem says “consistent sub-second at thousands of TPS.”

On-demand
  • Variable traffic, dev/test, bursty chat
  • No upfront capacity planning
  • May hit shared throttling at extreme scale
Provisioned
  • Steady production GenAI load
  • Latency SLA with known token rate
  • Cost optimization at high sustained volume
Common trap gotcha

Provisioned for a POC. Reserved throughput is wrong when traffic is unknown — on-demand until volume stabilizes. Also: model must be available in Region; cross-Region invoke adds latency and data-residency risk.

03 Regions & availability

Not every FM exists in every Region. Stems citing data residency (EU, HIPAA) → invoke in-region; if a model isn’t offered, pick an available alternative or enable cross-Region inference only when the stem allows data movement.

Ledger Bank needs 5k requests/minute with stable p99 latency on the same FM 24/7. Traffic is predictable for a year. Pricing model?

Right. Steady high QPS + latency SLA → provisioned. On-demand can throttle; EC2 DIY violates least-ops GenAI stems.

04 Exam rapid-fire

How do you pick between two Bedrock models on the exam?core
Model answer

Smallest model that meets quality, latency, and Region constraints — then compare on-demand vs provisioned from traffic pattern.

Say it out loud say it

"Right-size the model first; provisioned only when traffic is steady and SLA is tight."

On-demand vs provisioned in one line?warm-up
Model answer

On-demand = pay per use, spiky OK; provisioned = reserved capacity for predictable high-volume production.

"Spiky → on-demand; always-on high QPS → provisioned."

Stem: “Data must stay in eu-west-1.” What do you verify?stretch
Model answer

Chosen FM is offered in eu-west-1; invoke and store artifacts in that Region — no silent cross-Region routing unless stem permits.

"Residency → in-Region model and invoke; don't assume every FM is everywhere."
