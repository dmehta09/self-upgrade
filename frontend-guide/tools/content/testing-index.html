Testing — the trophy

The old “testing pyramid” over-rewarded brittle unit tests. The modern shape is a trophy: a base of static typing, a big middle of integration tests, fewer units, and a thin top of end-to-end. The rule under all of it — test behavior, not implementation.

00 The shape: a trophy, not a pyramid

Lean on the layer that gives the most confidence per second: lots of integration, a thin layer of e2e, and TypeScript catching whole classes of bugs for free.

Integration tests — a component or feature exercised the way a user does — are the sweet spot: realistic enough to catch real bugs, fast enough to run constantly. Units cover tricky pure logic; e2e guards a few critical flows end to end.

Think of it like… analogy

Think of inspecting a car. Checking each bolt in isolation (unit tests) proves little about whether it drives. A test drive around the block (integration) tells you most of what matters, fast. A full cross-country road trip (e2e) is the ultimate proof — but you don’t do one before every commit. The trophy spends effort where confidence is cheapest.
