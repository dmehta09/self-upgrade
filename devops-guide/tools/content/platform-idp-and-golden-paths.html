IDPs & golden paths.

Platform engineering turns "file a ticket, wait a week" into self-serve defaults. An Internal Developer Platform (IDP) plus golden paths make the secure, observable way to ship also the easiest way to ship.

01 What an IDP actually is

An Internal Developer Platform is the product your platform team ships to product teams: self-serve scaffolding, paved infra, and guardrails — so developers provision and deploy without opening a ticket for every cluster, pipeline, or DNS record.

It's not "more YAML docs." It's a curated interface (portal + APIs + templates) over the messy underlying cloud, with platform engineers owning the paved road the way a product team owns a feature.

In plain English analogy

Ticket ops is a hotel front desk: every towel request is a form and a wait. An IDP is a well-stocked room with a keycard — the paved options are already there, so you self-serve. Golden paths are the house-recommended routes on the map; you can bushwhack, but the signed trail is faster and safer.

Self-service
Developers create services, envs, and pipelines from templates — no waiting on a human queue for the common path.
Golden path / paved road
The blessed, documented way to build and run a service (CI shape, deploy, observability hooks, security defaults).
Guardrails
Policy and defaults (IAM scopes, PSA, required scans) baked in so the easy path is also the compliant path.
Escape hatches
Rare cases can leave the path — but they cost more and need explicit ownership, not silent snowflakes.

02 From ticket ops to paved road

The shift is cultural and technical: platform teams treat developers as customers, measure adoption, and invest in templates that encode how your company ships — instead of answering the same Slack question forever.

Ticket / hero ops
  • Every env change is a queue item.
  • Knowledge lives in a few experts' heads.
  • Security bolted on late, inconsistently.
  • Lead time dominates DORA metrics.
Golden-path IDP
  • Common paths are self-serve templates.
  • Defaults encode company standards once.
  • Scans, OIDC, and SLOs come for free.
  • Platform team measures adoption like a product.

03 What you put on the paved road

A useful golden path is opinionated about the happy path and silent about trivia. Start with the highest-traffic journeys.

CapabilityOn the golden path
Create a serviceTemplate → repo + CI + deploy config + catalog entry.
Ship to prodSame artifact through envs; OIDC roles; protected environments.
Run safelyDefault logging/metrics hooks; on-call ownership in the catalog.
Stay compliantRequired scans, least-privilege IAM, PSA-friendly pod specs.
Common trap gotcha

Building a portal without golden paths is just a pretty ticket form. Building paths nobody adopts is an empty highway. Measure time-to-first-deploy and % of services on the paved road — if both are bad, fix the product, don't mandate the portal.

What makes a "golden path" different from a wiki page of best practices?

Right. Docs alone don't change behavior. Golden paths win when scaffolding, CI, and defaults make compliance and good ops cheaper than inventing a snowflake.

04 Interview rapid-fire

What is platform engineering, and how does it relate to DevOps?warm-up
Model answer

DevOps is the culture and practices for shipping and running software together. Platform engineering is how larger orgs productize that: a platform team builds an IDP and golden paths so product teams self-serve the DevOps habits (CI, deploy, security, ownership) instead of reinventing them — or waiting on tickets.

Say it out loud say it

"DevOps is the goal; platform engineering builds the self-serve product that makes those habits the default — golden paths instead of ticket queues."

Ticket ops vs self-service — what would you change first?core
Model answer

Find the highest-volume tickets (new service, new env, deploy access) and turn those into templates + automation with guardrails. Publish them in an IDP so the paved road is one click. Keep escape hatches for special cases, but make them deliberate. Measure lead time and adoption so you're building a product, not a mandate.

Say it out loud say it

"I'd productize the top ticket types into golden-path templates — self-serve with defaults for CI, IAM, and observability — then measure how many teams actually use them."

What belongs on a golden path vs an escape hatch?stretch
Model answer

On the path: anything most services need the same way — repo layout, CI gates, OIDC deploy, catalog ownership, baseline monitoring hooks, PSA-safe pod defaults. Escape hatches: unusual runtimes, custom networking, or one-off compliance regimes — allowed with ownership and review, not as silent snowflakes that become the real standard.

Say it out loud say it

"Common journeys get templates and defaults; rare exceptions get an escape hatch with explicit ownership — never undocumented forks that become the de facto platform."
