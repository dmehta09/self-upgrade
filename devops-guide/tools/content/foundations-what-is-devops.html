What is DevOps?

DevOps is a culture and a set of practices that shorten and de-risk the loop from writing code to running it in production. It is not a job title you hire or a tool you buy.

01 A culture, not a product

DevOps = shared ownership of the whole delivery loop, with automation and fast feedback so changes ship small, often, and safely.

It tears down the old split where developers wrote code and a separate ops team ran it. The aim is one team accountable from idea to production — and back, because operating teaches you what to build next.

In plain English analogy

Picture a restaurant where the cooks (dev) and the waiters (ops) blame each other: "the kitchen sent it out wrong" vs "you took the order wrong." DevOps is making one team own the dish from kitchen to table — they taste it, serve it, hear the complaint, and fix the recipe.

02 The wall of confusion

Before DevOps, devs were rewarded for change (ship features) and ops for stability (don't break prod). Those incentives fight. Code got "thrown over the wall," and when it broke, each side blamed the other.

Wall of confusion
  • Dev throws a release over to ops, then moves on.
  • Ops gets a 2am page for code they never saw.
  • "Works on my machine" vs "your config is wrong."
  • Deploys are rare, huge, and terrifying.
Shared ownership
  • One team plans, builds, ships, and runs the service.
  • The author of the code is on the hook when it pages.
  • Same automated path to every environment.
  • Deploys are small, frequent, and boring.

"You build it, you run it" — Amazon's phrasing — collapses the wall by making the people who write a service also operate it.

Common trap gotcha

Renaming the Ops team to "the DevOps team" and routing all deploys through them just rebuilds the wall with a new sign on it. DevOps is a way of working that the whole org adopts — not a single hire, a silo, or a product license.

03 CALMS — the five pillars

CALMS is the standard checklist for "are we actually doing DevOps?" It is culture-first: tools alone change nothing if the incentives and ownership don't move.

Culture
Shared goals and blameless learning. Dev and ops win or lose together, not against each other.
Automation
Build, test, and deploy by machine, not by hand. Humans approve; robots execute repeatably.
Lean
Small batches, less work-in-progress. Tiny changes flow fast and are easy to debug when one breaks.
Measurement
Decide with data — delivery metrics like lead time and change-fail rate (see DORA), not gut feel.
Sharing
Open knowledge: runbooks, postmortems, dashboards. No heroes hoarding "how prod really works."

A company buys a CI/CD tool and a Kubernetes platform but keeps separate dev and ops teams with conflicting goals. Are they "doing DevOps"?

Right. The "C" in CALMS comes first for a reason. Tooling without shared ownership and blameless culture is just expensive automation bolted onto the old wall.

04 DevOps vs SRE vs Platform Engineering

These overlap and interviewers love the distinction. DevOps is the philosophy; SRE is one concrete implementation of it; Platform Engineering is the team-and-product approach that makes the philosophy easy to follow at scale.

 What it isKey idea
DevOpsA culture + practices to shorten and de-risk dev→ops.Shared ownership; ship small and often. Deliberately not prescriptive.
SREGoogle's concrete, prescriptive way to do DevOps.Error budgets, SLOs, and cutting toil. Reliability is engineered with hard numbers.
Platform Eng.A team that builds an Internal Developer Platform (IDP) as a product.Devs self-serve safely — golden paths, paved roads — instead of reinventing infra each time.

A common one-liner: "SRE is what you get when you treat operations as a software problem; class SRE implements interface DevOps." Platform Engineering then packages those reliable patterns so every team gets them by default.

Pro tip tip

Don't claim they're the same. Say DevOps is the goal, SRE is a prescriptive method with measurable reliability, and Platform Engineering builds the self-serve product (often on Backstage) that scales those good habits across many teams.

05 Interview rapid-fire

What is DevOps?warm-up
Model answer

DevOps is a culture and set of practices that shorten and de-risk the loop from writing code to running it — through shared ownership, automation, and fast feedback. It's summarized by CALMS (Culture, Automation, Lean, Measurement, Sharing). It is not a job title or a tool you buy.

Say it out loud say it

"DevOps is one team owning the whole loop from code to production and back, with automation and feedback so we ship small and often. It's a culture — CALMS — not a tool or a title."

DevOps vs SRE vs Platform Engineering — how do they relate?core
Model answer

DevOps is the philosophy. SRE is Google's prescriptive implementation of it — error budgets, SLOs, and relentless toil reduction, treating ops as a software problem. Platform Engineering builds an Internal Developer Platform as a product so devs self-serve safely on golden paths, scaling those good practices across teams.

Say it out loud say it

"DevOps is the goal; SRE is a concrete recipe with error budgets and SLOs; Platform Engineering builds the self-serve platform that makes those habits the default for every team."

Follow-ups to expect
  • What's an error budget? (The allowed unreliability under an SLO — spend it on shipping, freeze when it's gone.)
  • What is toil? (Manual, repetitive, automatable ops work with no lasting value.)
Is "DevOps engineer" a real role?stretch
Model answer

Strictly, DevOps is a culture, not a person — so "DevOps engineer" is an industry shorthand, not a pure definition. In practice the title means an engineer who builds the automation, pipelines, and infrastructure that let teams practice DevOps. The red flag is a "DevOps team" that's just renamed Ops handling all deploys — that recreates the wall.

Say it out loud say it

"DevOps is a culture, so the title is a shorthand. As a DevOps engineer I build the pipelines and infra that let product teams own and run their own services — not a gatekeeper who deploys for everyone."
