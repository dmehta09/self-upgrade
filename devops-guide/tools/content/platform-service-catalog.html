Platform Engineering · service catalog

Service catalog & developer portals.

A catalog answers "what runs here, who owns it, and how do I stand up another one?" Portals like Backstage turn that inventory into a product: ownership, docs, scorecards, and templates in one place.

01 Why inventories fail without a catalog

Spreadsheets and tribal knowledge don't survive org growth. A service catalog is the living registry of software components with owners, lifecycle metadata, and links to runbooks — the API for "who pages when this burns?"

In plain English analogy

Without a catalog, production is a city with no street signs — firefighters arrive and argue whose building it is. The catalog is the property registry: address, owner, emergency contact, and the permit that says how it was built.

Component
A service, library, website, or data pipeline registered with a unique name.
Owner
A team (not a person) responsible for on-call and changes — usually via a group / CODEOWNERS link.
Annotations
Links out: repo, CI, dashboards, SLO doc, runbook, pager duty.
Scorecards
Automated checks (coverage, vuln SLA, required probes) that grade how "golden" a service is.

02 Portals: Backstage-shaped products

Developer portals wrap the catalog with Software Templates (scaffold a golden-path service), TechDocs, and plugins (CI status, Kubernetes, scorecards). Backstage is the common open-source shape; cloud vendors ship similar hubs.

Wiki + Slack archaeology

  • Ownership is "ask in #ops."
  • Every new service reinvented CI YAML.
Catalog-backed portal
  • Entity YAML in the repo keeps the catalog honest.
  • Templates stamp ownership + obs hooks on day one.
  • Scorecards make standards visible, not preachy.
catalog-info.yaml
apiVersion: backstage.io/v1alpha1
kind: Component
metadata:
  name: checkout-api
  annotations:
    github.com/project-slug: acme/checkout-api
    pagerduty.com/service-id: PXXXX
spec:
  type: service
  lifecycle: production
  owner: group:payments
  system: commerce

03 Adoption beats features

Ship the thinnest catalog that answers ownership and "create service," then add scorecards. Measure: % of prod services registered, template adoption, mean time to first deploy from template.

Templates are how IDPs & golden paths get into every repo. The catalog is how you find them again at 2 a.m.
