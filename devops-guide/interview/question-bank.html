Interview prep · question bank

The DevOps question bank.

50+ of the questions that actually come up in DevOps loops, grouped by theme, each with a tight model answer. Read the answer, then close the page and say it back in your own words.

01 DevOps culture, DORA & SRE

What is DevOps, in one breath?warm-up
Model answer

A culture and set of practices that shorten and de-risk the loop from writing code to running it in production — through shared ownership, automation, and fast feedback. Summarized by CALMS (Culture, Automation, Lean, Measurement, Sharing). It's not a job title or a tool you buy.

What are the four DORA metrics?core
Model answer

Two for velocity — deployment frequency and lead time for changes — and two for stability — change-failure rate and failed-deployment recovery time (MTTR). Together they rate teams Elite → Low. The key insight is that speed and stability rise together; they're not a trade-off.

Say it out loud say it

"Deploy frequency and lead time measure speed; change-fail rate and recovery time measure stability. Elite teams are strong on all four — fast and safe go together."

DevOps vs SRE vs Platform Engineering?core
Model answer

DevOps is the philosophy. SRE is Google's prescriptive implementation — error budgets, SLOs, cutting toil, treating ops as a software problem. Platform Engineering builds an Internal Developer Platform as a product so teams self-serve on golden paths. A neat line: "class SRE implements interface DevOps."

Say it out loud say it

"DevOps is the goal; SRE is a concrete recipe with error budgets and SLOs; Platform Engineering builds the self-serve platform that makes those habits the default."

What is an error budget, and what does it unlock?stretch
Model answer

It's the allowed unreliability under an SLO — e.g. a 99.9% target leaves ~0.1% to "spend." While budget remains, you ship features freely; when it's exhausted, you freeze risky changes and pour effort into reliability. It turns the dev-vs-ops "stability vs speed" fight into a shared, data-driven dial.

What is "toil," and why minimize it?warm-up
Model answer

Toil is manual, repetitive, automatable operational work that scales with service size and creates no lasting value — restarting things by hand, copy-pasting deploys. SRE caps it (often ~50% of time) and automates it away, because toil burns people out and crowds out engineering. Eliminating toil is a core reason DevOps automates relentlessly.

Where's the line between DevOps and observability?core
Model answer

DevOps owns the delivery and operations process — pipelines, infra, deploys, incident response, the human loop. Observability is the telemetry that tells you whether the system is healthy: metrics, traces, logs, dashboards, and SLO burn-rate alerting. They meet at deploy health checks and alerting philosophy; the deep telemetry mechanics live in a dedicated observability discipline.

Heads up scope

PromQL, OpenTelemetry, Grafana, and SLO math sit in the companion Observability guide — keep this answer at the boundary, not in the internals.

02 Linux & networking

A Linux box is out of disk. How do you find what's eating it?core
Model answer

Start with df -h to see which mount is full, then du -sh /* (or du -xh / | sort -h | tail) to drill into the biggest directories. Watch for the classic trap: a deleted-but-still-open file held by a process — df shows full while du doesn't; find it with lsof | grep deleted and restart the holder.

What do the numbers in chmod 644 mean?warm-up
Model answer

Three octal digits for owner / group / other, where read=4, write=2, execute=1. So 644 = owner read+write (6), group and other read-only (4). A script needs 755 (owner full, others read+execute); a secret should be 600 (owner only).

A service won't respond on port 8080. Walk me through diagnosing it.core
Model answer

Layer by layer: is the process up (ps/systemctl status)? Is it actually listening (ss -ltnp | grep 8080)? Can you reach it locally (curl localhost:8080)? Then from outside — DNS resolves, then the firewall: on AWS, the security group inbound rule and any NACL. Work up the stack until one layer fails.

Say it out loud say it

"Process up? Listening on the port with ss? Reachable on localhost? Then DNS, then the security group — I move up the stack one layer at a time until something fails."

Difference between a process signal SIGTERM and SIGKILL?warm-up
Model answer

SIGTERM (15) politely asks a process to shut down — it can catch it, flush, and exit cleanly. SIGKILL (9) is uncatchable; the kernel kills it instantly with no cleanup. This matters in containers: orchestrators send SIGTERM, wait a grace period, then SIGKILL — so your app should handle SIGTERM to drain connections.

What's the difference between a /24 and a /16 CIDR block?core
Model answer

The number is how many leading bits are fixed as the network prefix. /24 fixes 24 bits, leaving 8 for hosts — 256 addresses. /16 leaves 16 host bits — 65,536 addresses. Bigger suffix range = more hosts. On AWS you'd give a VPC a /16 and carve subnets as /24s.

DNS resolves but the connection hangs — where do you look?stretch
Model answer

DNS is fine, so it's reachability or the listener. Check routing and firewalls: a hang (vs a fast refusal) usually means packets are being silently dropped — a security group / NACL with no matching allow, or a missing route to a NAT/IGW. A connection refused instead means the port is reachable but nothing is listening.

03 Git & CI/CD

CI vs CD vs continuous deployment?warm-up
Model answer

CI = every change is auto-built and tested against main. Continuous delivery = every green build is auto-packaged and always ready to release, but a human approves prod. Continuous deployment = that final push is automatic too — green reaches prod with no gate.

Say it out loud say it

"CI keeps main always-buildable. Delivery keeps it always-shippable with a human approval. Deployment removes even that click."

Trunk-based development vs Git Flow — when each?core
Model answer

Trunk-based: short-lived branches merged to main daily, hidden behind feature flags — pairs naturally with CI and high deploy frequency. Git Flow: long-lived develop/release/hotfix branches, suited to versioned/on-prem software with scheduled releases. For a service shipping continuously, trunk-based wins; Git Flow's long branches fight CI.

Say it out loud say it

"Trunk-based for services shipping continuously — small PRs, flags, daily merges. Git Flow only for versioned releases; its long branches defeat the point of CI."

Merge vs rebase — what's the difference and the danger?core
Model answer

Merge keeps full history and adds a merge commit; rebase replays your commits on top of the target for a linear history but rewrites commit hashes. The golden rule: never rebase a branch others have pulled — you change shared history and force everyone into conflicts. Rebase locally, merge to share.

A teammate pushed a secret to the repo. What now?stretch
Model answer

Treat it as leaked the moment it's pushed: rotate the credential immediately — that's the real fix. Then purge it from history (git filter-repo / BFG) and force-push, but assume bots already scraped it. Going forward, add pre-commit secret scanning and push secrets through Secrets Manager / SSM, never the repo.

Say it out loud say it

"Rotate the secret first — purging history doesn't un-leak it. Then scrub the history, and add pre-commit scanning so it can't happen again."

Your pipeline takes 30 minutes. How do you speed it up?core
Model answer

Measure which stage dominates, then attack it: cache dependencies and Docker layers, run jobs in parallel (a test matrix), fail fast (lint before the slow suite), and push heavy end-to-end tests to a nightly or post-merge run. Aim for PR feedback under ~10 minutes — past that, people stop waiting and merge on faith.

How should CI authenticate to AWS — and what's the anti-pattern?stretch
Model answer

Use OIDC: the CI provider issues a short-lived token and the pipeline assumes an IAM role scoped to just what it needs (e.g. ECR push). No long-lived AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY stored as a secret — nothing to leak or rotate. Static keys in CI are the classic security-review finding.

Say it out loud say it

"Federate with OIDC and assume a least-privilege role per pipeline. Short-lived creds beat long-lived keys you have to store, rotate, and pray nobody logs."

04 Containers (Docker)

Container vs VM — what's the real difference?warm-up
Model answer

A VM virtualizes hardware and runs a full guest OS with its own kernel — heavy, slow to boot, strong isolation. A container virtualizes the OS: processes share the host kernel, isolated by namespaces and cgroups — light, fast, dense. Containers package the app + deps; the kernel is borrowed, not shipped.

What does a multi-stage Dockerfile buy you?core
Model answer

You build in a fat stage (compilers, dev deps) then COPY --from only the final artifact into a tiny runtime image. The result is much smaller and far safer — no build tools, no source, smaller attack surface. Pair it with a distroless or -slim base and a non-root user.

Say it out loud say it

"Build in a heavy stage, then copy just the binary into a slim runtime. Smaller image, no compilers in prod, smaller attack surface — and run as non-root."

Why is layer order important for build caching?core
Model answer

Docker caches each layer and invalidates everything below the first changed line. So copy your dependency manifest and install before copying source — deps rarely change, so that expensive layer stays cached while your code edits only bust the cheap final layers. Reversing it reinstalls everything on every commit.

Why should containers not run as root?core
Model answer

The container shares the host kernel, so a root process plus a container-escape or kernel bug can mean root on the host. Running as an unprivileged user (a USER line, read-only root filesystem, dropped capabilities) shrinks the blast radius. It's a baseline DevSecOps control and a near-universal interview checkpoint.

CMD vs ENTRYPOINT?warm-up
Model answer

ENTRYPOINT is the fixed executable the container always runs; CMD is the default arguments, easily overridden at docker run. Common pattern: ENTRYPOINT is your binary, CMD is its default flags. Both should use exec form (JSON array) so signals like SIGTERM reach the process directly.

An image is 1.2 GB. How do you shrink it?stretch
Model answer

Use a multi-stage build to drop build tooling, switch to a smaller base (distroless / -slim / Alpine), combine and clean up RUN layers (remove caches in the same layer), and add a .dockerignore so you don't copy .git/node_modules. Then scan with Trivy — smaller images also have fewer CVEs.

05 Kubernetes

Explain Kubernetes in one sentence.warm-up
Model answer

A control loop that continuously drives the cluster's actual state toward a declared desired state — you describe what you want (3 replicas of this image), and controllers reconcile reality to match, rescheduling on failure. It's declarative and self-healing, not a script you run.

Pod vs Deployment vs Service — how do they relate?core
Model answer

A Pod is one or more co-located containers — the smallest unit, and disposable. A Deployment manages a ReplicaSet to keep N identical pods running and handles rolling updates. A Service gives that shifting set of pods one stable virtual IP / DNS name and load-balances across them. Deployment manages lifecycle; Service manages networking.

Say it out loud say it

"Pod = the running unit, disposable. Deployment keeps N of them alive and rolls updates. Service is the stable address that load-balances across whichever pods exist."

Liveness vs readiness probes?core
Model answer

Liveness = "is the process healthy?" — fail it and the kubelet restarts the pod. Readiness = "should it receive traffic yet?" — fail it and it's pulled from the Service endpoints but not restarted. Mixing them up is a classic bug: a too-aggressive liveness probe kills a pod that's merely slow to warm up.

Say it out loud say it

"Liveness restarts an unhealthy pod; readiness just stops sending it traffic. The trap is a trigger-happy liveness probe that keeps killing a pod that's only slow to start."

Requests vs limits for CPU and memory?core
Model answer

Requests are the guaranteed reservation the scheduler uses to place a pod; limits are the hard ceiling. Over a CPU limit the pod is throttled; over a memory limit it's OOMKilled. Set requests realistically (under-requesting causes noisy-neighbor contention) and treat a CrashLoop with reason OOMKilled as "raise the memory limit or fix the leak."

ConfigMap vs Secret — and is a Secret actually secret?stretch
Model answer

Both inject config (env vars or mounted files); a Secret is meant for sensitive values. But by default Secrets are only base64-encoded, not encrypted — anyone with API/etcd access can read them. So enable encryption at rest for etcd, lock down RBAC, and for production pull from an external store (AWS Secrets Manager via the CSI driver / External Secrets).

How does a pod get a stable identity for AWS access on EKS?stretch
Model answer

Via IAM Roles for Service Accounts (IRSA) or the newer EKS Pod Identity: a Kubernetes service account is mapped to an IAM role, so the pod's SDK gets short-lived, least-privilege AWS credentials automatically — no node-wide instance role, no static keys baked into the image. It's the EKS answer to "least privilege per workload."

HPA vs VPA vs Cluster Autoscaler / Karpenter?stretch
Model answer

HPA scales pod count on CPU/memory/custom metrics. VPA tunes a pod's requests/limits (resizes it). The Cluster Autoscaler (or Karpenter on AWS) adds/removes nodes when pods can't be scheduled or sit idle. HPA handles load within the cluster; the node autoscaler grows the cluster itself.

06 Infrastructure as Code (Terraform)

What problem does Infrastructure as Code solve?warm-up
Model answer

It replaces click-ops and snowflake servers with version-controlled, reviewable, repeatable infra definitions. You get the same environment every time, a diff before you change anything, and an audit trail in Git. It kills configuration drift and makes disaster recovery a re-apply.

What is the Terraform state file, and why is it sensitive?core
Model answer

State is Terraform's map from your config to real cloud resource IDs — how it knows what already exists and what to change. It can contain secrets in plaintext, so store it remote (S3 with encryption + a DynamoDB lock, or Terraform Cloud), never in Git, and lock it so two applies can't clobber each other.

Say it out loud say it

"State maps config to real resources. It can hold secrets, so it lives in encrypted S3 with a DynamoDB lock — remote and locked, never committed to Git."

Difference between plan and apply?warm-up
Model answer

plan is a dry run: it diffs desired config against state and shows what it would create, change, or destroy — no changes made. apply executes that plan. The discipline is to review the plan (ideally as a PR artifact) and watch for unexpected destroy/replace lines before approving.

Someone changed infra in the AWS console. What happens on next apply?core
Model answer

That's drift. Terraform compares state to reality, sees the manual change, and on the next plan proposes to revert it back to what the code says — overwriting the console edit. The fix is to make the change in code, or import existing resources, and to prevent console edits to managed infra.

Terraform vs OpenTofu vs CloudFormation/CDK?core
Model answer

Terraform is cloud-agnostic HCL, now under HashiCorp's BSL license. OpenTofu is its open-source (MPL) fork after that relicensing — interviews like the license-split awareness. CloudFormation is AWS-native YAML/JSON; CDK lets you define that infra in Python/TypeScript. Pick Terraform/OpenTofu for multi-cloud or a shared workflow, CloudFormation/CDK for deep AWS-only integration.

How do you structure Terraform for multiple environments?stretch
Model answer

Factor shared infra into reusable modules, then give each environment its own root config and separate state file (its own S3 key) so a staging apply can never touch prod. Vary inputs with per-env .tfvars. The key principle is state isolation per environment — blast-radius containment beats one giant state.

07 AWS for DevOps

Security group vs NACL?core
Model answer

A security group is stateful and attached to an ENI/instance — allow rules only; return traffic is auto-permitted. A NACL is stateless and attached to a subnet — explicit allow and deny, evaluated by rule number, and you must allow return traffic separately. SGs are your primary control; NACLs are a coarse subnet-level backstop.

Say it out loud say it

"Security groups are stateful, per-instance, allow-only — return traffic is automatic. NACLs are stateless, per-subnet, with deny rules. I rely on SGs and use NACLs as a coarse backstop."

Public vs private subnet — what makes the difference?core
Model answer

It's the route table. A public subnet has a route to an Internet Gateway, so its resources can have public IPs. A private subnet has no IGW route; for outbound-only internet (e.g. pulling updates) it routes through a NAT Gateway sitting in a public subnet. App servers and databases belong in private subnets.

EC2 vs ECS/Fargate vs Lambda — how do you choose?core
Model answer

EC2 = full OS control, you patch and scale it — legacy or stateful workloads. ECS/Fargate = containers with no servers to manage — the default for most services. Lambda = no servers, pay-per-request — spiky or event-driven work, but mind cold starts. Trade control for less ops as you move down the list.

Say it out loud say it

"EC2 for control or legacy, Fargate for most containerized services, Lambda for spiky event-driven work. The trade is less control for less ops as you go serverless."

What does an IAM role give you over an access key?core
Model answer

A role grants temporary, auto-rotated credentials that a trusted entity assumes — an EC2 instance role, an EKS service account (IRSA), or a CI pipeline via OIDC. No long-lived secret to store, leak, or rotate by hand. Long-lived access keys are the thing security reviews flag first.

Say it out loud say it

"A role hands out short-lived, auto-rotated creds to a trusted identity — instance role, IRSA, or OIDC for CI. Nothing static to leak. Keys are the anti-pattern."

Why deploy across multiple Availability Zones?warm-up
Model answer

An AZ is an isolated datacenter; spreading instances across two or three means one AZ failing doesn't take the service down. You put an ALB in front of an Auto Scaling Group spanning AZs, and run RDS Multi-AZ for a standby in another zone. Region-level resilience is a separate, harder problem.

Where should application secrets live on AWS?stretch
Model answer

In Secrets Manager (supports rotation) or SSM Parameter Store (SecureString, cheaper), encrypted with KMS, and fetched at deploy/runtime via an IAM role — never baked into the image, env files in Git, or Terraform state. Grant read access narrowly per service.

08 Deploy strategies & GitOps

Compare blue-green and canary deployments.core
Model answer

Blue-green stands up a full second environment, then flips all traffic at once — instant rollback (flip back), but double the infra. Canary shifts a small % to the new version, watches health, and widens gradually — tiny blast radius and no full duplicate, but slower and needs good metrics. Canary for high-traffic services; blue-green when you need an instant, clean cutover.

Say it out loud say it

"Blue-green = two full environments, flip all at once, instant rollback but double cost. Canary = trickle traffic to v2 and widen if healthy — small blast radius, slower."

What's the catch with a rolling deploy and a database change?stretch
Model answer

During a rolling deploy both versions run at once, so the schema must stay backward-compatible with the old code. Use expand/contract: first deploy an additive change (new nullable column), migrate, switch the app to use it, then a later release removes the old path. A destructive migration mid-roll breaks the pods still on the old version.

What is GitOps?core
Model answer

Git is the single source of truth for desired state, and a controller in the cluster (Argo CD / Flux) continuously reconciles reality to match the repo. It's pull-based: the cluster syncs itself rather than a pipeline pushing in. You get an audit trail, easy rollback (revert the commit), and drift correction for free.

Say it out loud say it

"Git is the desired state; Argo CD or Flux pulls it into the cluster and reconciles drift. Rollback is a git revert, and you get an audit trail for free."

Push-based vs pull-based deployment?core
Model answer

Push: a CI/CD pipeline holds cluster credentials and pushes changes in. Pull (GitOps): an in-cluster agent watches Git and pulls, so credentials stay inside the cluster and any drift is auto-corrected. Pull is more secure (no external system holds prod creds) and self-healing; push is simpler to start with.

Deploy vs release — and why decouple them?stretch
Model answer

Deploy = the code is running in prod; release = users can see it. A feature flag lets you deploy dark and flip on later, so launch timing and rollback become a toggle instead of a redeploy. It separates shipping risk from launch decisions — and a flag-off is your fastest rollback.

09 Reliability, security & behavioral

Walk me through how you'd handle a production incident.core
Model answer

Declare the incident and assign an incident commander to coordinate. Prioritize mitigation over root cause — roll back, flip a flag, or scale out to stop the bleeding first. Communicate status on a clear channel, then once stable, do a blameless postmortem to fix the systemic cause and update the runbook.

Why "blameless" postmortems?warm-up
Model answer

Because the goal is to fix the system, not punish a person. People hide mistakes when they fear blame, which buries the real causes; a blameless culture surfaces them, so you can add the guardrail that makes that failure impossible next time. You assume everyone acted reasonably given what they knew.

Say it out loud say it

"Blame makes people hide mistakes, which hides the real causes. Blameless surfaces them so we can fix the system — assume everyone did their best with what they knew."

What does "shift-left security" mean in a pipeline?core
Model answer

Move security checks early — into the developer's loop and CI — instead of a gate at the end. In practice: SAST and dependency/secret scanning on every PR, image scanning with Trivy before push, and an SBOM. Catching a vulnerable dependency at PR time is vastly cheaper than in production.

What's an SBOM and why does supply-chain security care?stretch
Model answer

A Software Bill of Materials (SPDX or CycloneDX) is a complete inventory of every component and version in your artifact. When the next Log4Shell-style CVE drops, an SBOM lets you answer "are we affected and where?" in minutes. Pair it with artifact signing (Sigstore/cosign) and provenance (SLSA) to prove what you ran is what you built.

What is the principle of least privilege, concretely on AWS?core
Model answer

Grant each identity only the permissions it needs, and nothing more. Concretely: scoped IAM policies (not *), a distinct role per service, short-lived credentials via roles/OIDC, and resource-level conditions. It limits blast radius — a compromised CI token that can only push to one ECR repo can't drain an S3 bucket.

Say it out loud say it

"Each identity gets exactly the permissions it needs — scoped IAM policies, a role per service, short-lived creds. So a leaked token can do one small thing, not own the account."

Behavioral: tell me about a time you broke production.warm-up
Model answer

Use STAR and own it without drama: the Situation, the Task, the specific Action you took to mitigate (rolled back, communicated), and the Result — including the systemic fix you added so it can't recur. Interviewers want ownership, a calm response, and evidence you turned the failure into a guardrail, not a scapegoat.

Behavioral: how do you push back on shipping something risky?core
Model answer

Frame it in terms of shared goals and data, not opinion: name the specific risk and blast radius, propose a safer path (canary, feature flag, more tests), and offer a way to ship the value without the risk. Show you can disagree, commit once a decision's made, and make the risk visible rather than block on principle.
