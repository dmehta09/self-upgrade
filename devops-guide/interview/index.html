Interview prep · the method

How DevOps interviews work.

A DevOps loop tests less "do you know the flag" and more "can you reason about a system under pressure." This hub gives you the rounds, the shape of a strong answer, and the practice surfaces to drill them.

01 The rounds, and what each probes

Most DevOps loops in 2026 run 4–5 rounds. The early ones check fundamentals; the later ones check judgement under ambiguity — trade-offs, failure, and how you communicate.

RoundWhat it looks likeWhat it's really scoring (basic → pro)
Recruiter screen15–30 min chat: your stack, scope, why you're looking.Can you describe what you actually own? Basic: list tools. Pro: frame impact ("cut deploy time / MTTR").
Technical / fundamentalsRapid-fire on Linux, networking, Git, Docker, K8s, IaC, AWS.Crisp mental models. Basic: recite a definition. Pro: define + give an AWS example + name the trade-off.
Systems / scenario design"Design a CI/CD pipeline" or "stand up prod infra on AWS."Clarify first, then reason out loud. Basic: one happy path. Pro: failure modes, rollback, security, cost.
Troubleshooting / debugging"This rollout is stuck / the deploy is failing — find it."A calm, ordered method. Basic: guess. Pro: form a hypothesis, narrow with evidence, state what you'd check next.
BehavioralIncident you handled, a conflict, a risky change.Ownership + a blameless lens. Basic: "we fixed it." Pro: STAR, your specific role, what you changed afterwards.

02 The shape of a strong answer

Weak answers stop at the definition. Strong answers follow a four-beat formula that signals real experience in about three sentences.

1

Define the term

One precise sentence. Show you actually know what it is, not just that you've heard it.

2

Give a concrete example or AWS service

Ground it. "We did this on ECS / ALB / Terraform…" beats abstractions every time.

3

Name the trade-off

Nothing is free. Stating the cost ("slower rollback," "more moving parts") shows judgement, not just recall.

4

Mention the gotcha

The failure mode or "thing people get wrong" is what separates a 3-year engineer from someone who just read a blog.

Worked mini-example — "What's a rolling deploy?"

Define
"Replace instances of the old version with the new a few at a time, so the service stays up throughout."
Example
"It's the default for a Kubernetes Deployment and for an ECS rolling update."
Trade-off
"No downtime, but rollback is slow — you have to roll forward/back pod by pod — and both versions run at once."
Gotcha
"That overlap means the DB schema and API must stay backward-compatible during the roll."
Pro tip tip

Default to define → example → trade-off → gotcha on every fundamentals question. It keeps you concise, stops you from rambling, and lands the two beats (trade-off + gotcha) that most candidates skip.

04 Interview technique itself

How do you approach a question you don't know the answer to?core
Model answer

Say what you do know and reason out loud from first principles — interviewers score the thinking, not just the recall. State your assumptions explicitly ("I haven't used X, but it sounds like Y, which I'd handle by…"), and offer how you'd find the real answer. Bluffing a fake fact is the only true fail.

Say it out loud say it

"I haven't hit that exact case, so let me reason about it: here's what it resembles, here's my assumption, and here's how I'd verify it. Then I'd check the docs to confirm."

How do you structure a "design a deployment pipeline" answer?stretch
Model answer

Clarify first — language, scale, target (EKS? ECS? Lambda?), team maturity — then sketch the flow: commit → CI build & test → push a versioned image to ECR → deploy to staging → gate → prod, with rollback and least-privilege auth (OIDC) called out. Talk through it left to right, and pause to name a trade-off at each gate. Don't dump the whole design before checking your assumptions.

Say it out loud say it

"First, a few clarifying questions — stack, scale, and where it runs. Then I'd walk the pipeline left to right: commit, CI, image to ECR, staging, a gate, prod — calling out how I'd roll back and how CI authenticates without long-lived keys."
