Deploy health → observability handoff.

A green pipeline means the artifact rolled out — not that users are happy. DevOps owns safe ship + rollback; observability owns SLOs, burn alerts, and the RED/USE signals that prove the deploy is healthy.

01 Where the handoff happens

After promote/sync, the pipeline's job is done when traffic is on the new version and automated health gates pass. Sustained correctness — error budgets, multi-window burns, deep dashboards — lives in the Observability guide.

In plain English analogy

Deploy is landing the plane; observability is air-traffic control watching altitude and fuel for the rest of the flight. The runway checklist (smoke tests, canary %) is DevOps; the continuous radar and fuel-burn math (SLOs, Alertmanager) is observability.

02 What deploy pipelines hand to obs

You don't re-teach PromQL here — you make sure every service ships with the hooks and contracts observability expects.

SLIs / SLOs
Availability & latency targets for the user journey you just deployed — see error budgets.
Burn-rate alerts
Fast + slow windows that page before the budget is gone — wired through Alertmanager.
RED (and USE)
Rate, Errors, Duration for request services; Utilization/Saturation/Errors for resources — RED / USE.

Where metrics/logs/traces land in your org — the observability stack.

03 Practical checklist after every prod ship

Treat "deploy succeeded" as incomplete until you've confirmed signals and rollback readiness. Automate what you can; don't rely on staring at a green GitHub check.

1

Confirm version & traffic

Right image digest / rollout step; canary % matches intent.

2

Watch RED + errors

Spike in error rate or latency p99 vs baseline — gate or rollback.

3

Respect the budget

If burn alerts fire, stop shipping and spend the budget on fix/rollback.

4

Know the revert

GitOps revert, previous task set, or feature-flag off — practiced, not theoretical.

Common trap gotcha

A pipeline that only checks "pods Ready" misses bad deploys that serve 500s with Ready=True. Health must include user-facing signals (RED/SLIs), not just kube probe success.

Your GitOps sync finished and pods are Ready, but the error-budget burn alert is paging. What failed?

Right. Ready ≠ good. The observability handoff exists precisely so user-impact signals can override a green sync.

04 Interview rapid-fire

What do you check after a production deploy?warm-up
Model answer

Confirm the intended version is serving traffic, watch RED (or golden signals) vs baseline, and ensure rollback is one command away. If we have SLOs, watch burn-rate alerts — a green pipeline isn't enough if error rate or latency blows the budget.

Say it out loud say it

"Version and traffic first, then RED and SLO burn — Ready pods aren't success. If burns fire, I roll back; I don't argue with the pipeline checkmark."

How do progressive delivery and SLOs connect?core
Model answer

Canary/analysis steps query the same SLIs the SLO is built on — error rate, latency. If the canary burns budget or fails RED thresholds, auto-rollback keeps blast radius small. Deploy tooling owns the traffic shift; observability owns the signal definitions and alert math.

Say it out loud say it

"Progressive delivery shifts traffic; SLOs define healthy. Analysis jobs query those SLIs — fail the canary and roll back before the whole fleet eats the budget."

Follow-ups to expect
  • Where do you learn error-budget math? (Observability · error budgets.)
  • Who pages? (Alertmanager routes — Alertmanager.)
Draw the boundary between DevOps and observability in an interview.stretch
Model answer

DevOps: pipelines, infra, release strategies, incident process, rollback mechanics. Observability: instrumentation, metrics/logs/traces, SLO/error-budget design, Prom/Alertmanager, the stack. The handoff is deploy health — we agree on RED/SLIs and alerts, then each side goes deep in its guide. Naming golden signals is fine; deriving burn-rate formulas belongs in observability.

Say it out loud say it

"I ship and roll back on this side; SLOs, RED, and Alertmanager live on the observability side. Deploy health is the contract between them."
