How to use this guide

This is a complete, visual tour of generative AI — built so you can understand it deeply and talk about it confidently in an interview. Every page leads with a plain-English picture, then gives you the precise, senior-sounding version. Here's how it all fits together so you get the most out of it.

01  Who this is for

You're an engineer — maybe a year or three in — who keeps hearing “RAG,” “embeddings,” “fine-tuning,” “agents,” “MCP,” and wants to actually understand them, not just nod along. Perhaps an interview is coming. This guide assumes you can read a little Python and have used an LLM chatbot, but assumes nothing about machine-learning theory.

Explained like you're ten — answered like you're senior analogy

Think of a great teacher who first tells you a little story so the idea clicks, and only then teaches you the exact words to use on the test. That's the whole method here: an everyday analogy to build intuition, then the crisp definition and the sentence you'd actually say to an interviewer.

02  How each page is built

Every concept page follows the same rhythm, so you always know where to look:

Analogy
An everyday picture that makes the idea obvious before any jargon.
Plain definition
The concept in one or two clear sentences.
Diagram or widget
Something to see or poke — a flow diagram, an animation, or a hands-on toy.
Trade-offs
A small table of when-to-use / when-not, because interviews live in trade-offs.
Say it out loud
The exact, senior-sounding sentence to give an interviewer. Look for the green tip box.
Gotcha
The trap people fall into — and how to avoid sounding naïve.
Self-check
A quick quiz and a couple of “could you answer this out loud?” questions.
Watch for two badges tip

IV marks an interview-critical idea — if you skim, don't skim these. NEW marks something that changed in 2025–2026, so older knowledge might be stale. We date anything that moves fast.

03  Make it stick

This isn't a wall of text — it's built to be used:

  • Mark lessons learned. Hit the button at the bottom of any page; your progress shows on the home dashboard, saved in your browser (no sign-in).
  • Search anything with / — jump straight to a concept, term, or interview question.
  • Play with the toys. Type into the tokenizer, drag sampling sliders, step through attention and diffusion — all running locally, no keys or internet.
  • Drill for the interview. The Interview prep module has a question bank, seven system-design case studies, cheat-sheets and flashcards.

04  What we cover — and what we skip

The guide is deliberately generative-AI-first. We go deep on what you'll be asked about: how LLMs work, prompting and context, retrieval and RAG, agents and tools, fine-tuning, serving, evaluation, safety, and the full multimodal story (image, audio, video). We bring in deep-learning ideas only where they're load-bearing for GenAI — transformers, attention, embeddings, diffusion — and skip generic ML (classical algorithms, training theory, vision classifiers) that won't come up in a GenAI interview.

Frameworks live next door note

We teach concepts generically, with plain Python. When you want the framework-specific “how” — LangChain, LangGraph, LangSmith — see the sibling Lang Stack guide. We'll point you there at the right moments instead of repeating it.

You can read in any order, but this sequence builds cleanly. Each module also stands alone if you need to cram one topic before an interview.

06  Quick check

Why does this guide teach concepts generically and link out for LangChain/LangGraph specifics?
Frameworks are useful, but they're an implementation detail. Interviewers probe whether you understand retrieval, tool-calling, and evaluation — ideas that outlast any one library. We point to the Lang Stack guide when you want the code.
“How would you describe your generative-AI knowledge?”Warm-up
Model answer

Frame it as the stack: you understand how LLMs work (tokens, attention, sampling), how to steer them (prompting, structured output, RAG, tools/agents), how to adapt them (fine-tuning), and how to ship them (serving, evaluation, safety, cost). Then name one thing you've actually built or studied deeply.

How to say it out loud say it

“I think about it as a stack: the model itself, how you steer it with prompting and retrieval, how you let it act with tools, and how you adapt, serve and evaluate it. I'm strongest on the RAG and serving side, and I've been going deep on agents.”
