GANs & VAEs IV

Before diffusion, two ideas ran the show. GANs staged a duel between a forger and a detective and made shockingly sharp images — when they didn't fall apart. VAEs learned to compress images into a tidy little code and back. Both faded for general generation, but you must know them cold: interviewers love the contrast, and one of them — the VAE — quietly lives inside every modern diffusion model.

01  GANs: counterfeiter vs detective

A GAN (Generative Adversarial Network) trains two networks against each other. The generator takes random noise and tries to produce a convincing image. The discriminator is shown a mix of real images and the generator's fakes and must answer one question: real or fake? They improve together — the generator getting better at faking, the discriminator at catching it.

A forger and a detective who train each other analogy

A counterfeiter prints fake banknotes; a detective learns to spot them. Every time the detective catches a flaw, the counterfeiter fixes it; every time a fake slips through, the detective sharpens up. After a long cat-and-mouse, the forger's notes are good enough to fool experts. The generator is that forger — and once training ends, you throw the detective away and keep the forger.

The two networks share one loss with opposite goals: the generator wants the discriminator to say “real,” the discriminator wants to be right. That tug-of-war is what “adversarial” means.

02  Why GANs faded: instability & mode collapse

A duel only produces good images if neither side runs away with it. In practice that balance is brittle, and two failure modes show up constantly:

Training instability
If the discriminator gets too good too fast, it rejects everything and the generator gets no useful signal (vanishing gradients); push the other way and quality oscillates. Getting a GAN to converge was famously finicky — a craft of careful tuning.
Mode collapse
The generator discovers a few outputs that reliably fool the judge and just makes those forever — e.g. one pose, a handful of faces. It wins the game while ignoring most of the data distribution. You asked for variety; you get clones.

Compare that to diffusion, which is trained on a stable reconstruction objective and is rewarded for covering the whole distribution. That contrast — fragile adversarial game vs stable per-step regression — is the heart of “why diffusion won.”

Mode collapse ≠ overfitting gotcha

Don't conflate them. Overfitting is memorizing training examples; mode collapse is producing too few distinct outputs regardless of input noise. A collapsed GAN can still make novel-looking images — there just aren't many kinds of them. The tell is low diversity, not memorization.

03  VAEs: encode → latent → decode

A VAE (Variational Autoencoder) takes the opposite tack — no duel. An encoder squeezes an image down into a small bundle of numbers called a latent code; a decoder tries to rebuild the original image from just that code. Train them together to reconstruct faithfully, and the latent becomes a compact summary of “what's in the picture.”

A sketch artist's shorthand analogy

A courtroom sketch artist can't copy every detail, so they capture the essence — face shape, posture, mood — in a few quick strokes. The encoder is that shorthand: it boils a megapixel image down to a handful of numbers. The decoder is an artist who can redraw a plausible full image from the shorthand. The “variational” twist forces the shorthand to be smooth and well-organized, so nearby codes decode to similar images.

The catch when used alone as a generator: the reconstruction objective averages over uncertainty, so sampled VAE images tend to look soft and blurry. Great structure, mushy detail. That's why pure VAEs lost the quality race — but their latent space turned out to be the gift that mattered.

04  The latent space that diffusion stole NEW

Here's the plot twist worth flagging in an interview. Diffusion didn't beat the VAE so much as recruit it. Running diffusion directly on millions of raw pixels is brutally expensive. Latent diffusion (the Stable-Diffusion idea) uses a VAE encoder to shrink the image into a small latent grid, runs the entire noisy-to-clean diffusion process in that compact latent space, then uses the VAE decoder to blow the result back up to full pixels.

A latent grid can be ~64× smaller than the pixel image (≈8× on each side), so diffusion does far less work. The VAE handles the costly pixel detail; diffusion handles the structure. We unpack this on How diffusion works.

So the VAE wasn't a dead end — it became the efficiency layer underneath the winner. When you hear “Stable Diffusion,” the “Stable” lineage and the “latent” trick both trace back to this VAE-plus-diffusion marriage.

05  Where GANs still win

“Diffusion won” is true for general text-to-image, but a single-pass GAN has one permanent advantage: speed. It produces an image in one forward pass, no iterative loop. That keeps GANs (and GAN-style one-step generators) alive wherever latency is king:

Super-resolution & upscaling
Sharpening or 4×-ing an image in one pass — common in photo tools and as a final step after diffusion.
Real-time & on-device effects
Face filters, style transfer, avatars where you can't wait for 20–50 diffusion steps.
Restoration
Face restoration and de-noising pipelines where a fast, sharp single shot beats a slow perfect one.

And the line keeps blurring: diffusion's own research borrows the “one fast pass” goal via consistency models, which distill many steps down to a few — chasing GAN-like speed without GAN-like instability.

How to say it out loud say it

“A GAN trains a generator against a discriminator — fast one-pass generation, but unstable and prone to mode collapse. A VAE encodes an image to a latent and decodes back — stable but blurry alone. Diffusion beat both for general generation by being stable and mode-covering, and it actually reuses the VAE's latent space to run efficiently. GANs still win where you need single-pass speed, like super-resolution.”

06  GAN vs diffusion — trade-offs

The comparison interviewers reach for. Keep the axes straight:

A clean one-liner: “GANs trade stability and diversity for one-pass speed; diffusion trades speed for stability, diversity, and controllability.”

07  Self-check

In a modern latent-diffusion image model, what job does the VAE do?
In latent diffusion the VAE is the on-ramp/off-ramp: its encoder compresses pixels to a small latent grid, diffusion does its many denoising steps there (cheap), and the decoder restores full pixels. There's no discriminator (that's GANs), and the per-step denoising is done by the diffusion network, not the VAE.
“Walk me through how a GAN trains.”Core
Model answer

Two networks. The generator maps random noise to a fake image; the discriminator classifies images as real or fake. We alternate: train the discriminator on real + fake batches to get better at spotting fakes, then train the generator to fool the current discriminator. Their losses oppose each other, so they co-evolve. At the end we keep the generator and discard the discriminator. The danger is imbalance — vanishing gradients if the discriminator dominates, or mode collapse if the generator finds a cheap way to win.

How to say it out loud say it

“Generator fakes, discriminator judges, they train in opposition until the fakes pass. Keep the generator at the end. Watch for instability and mode collapse.”

“Why does a VAE produce blurry images on its own?”Core
Model answer

Because it's trained to reconstruct with a likelihood/MSE-style objective and to keep its latent space smooth. When the model is unsure about fine detail, the loss-minimizing choice is to average the possibilities — and the average of many sharp possibilities is a blur. It nails global structure but smears high-frequency texture. Diffusion avoids this by sampling detail step by step rather than predicting one averaged image.

How to say it out loud say it

“Its reconstruction objective averages over uncertain detail, and averaging sharp options gives blur. Good structure, soft texture — which is why its latent is reused but its decoder alone isn't the generator.”
