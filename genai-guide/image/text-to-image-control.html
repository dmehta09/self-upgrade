Image & diffusion · steering the output

Conditioning & control IV NEW

A denoiser left alone just makes some plausible image. Conditioning is how you make it your image. Words steer it through CLIP-style text embeddings and cross-attention; pose, depth, and layout steer it through ControlNet and adapters; and existing images steer it through img2img, inpainting, and instruction edits. This page is the toolbox for controlling composition, not just content.

01  CLIP: putting words and pictures in one space NEW

For a prompt to steer pixels, text and images need to live in the same coordinate system. CLIP (Contrastive Language–Image Pre-training) does exactly that. It trains a text encoder and an image encoder together on millions of caption–image pairs, pulling matching pairs close in a shared embedding space and pushing mismatches apart. After training, “a photo of a corgi” and an actual corgi photo land near each other.

A bilingual dictionary for sight and language analogy

Imagine a map where every caption and every photo gets a pin, and the map was arranged so a caption's pin sits right next to the photo it describes. CLIP built that map. Now “red sports car” and pictures of red sports cars are neighbors — so a text prompt can point to a region of image-space, and the generator knows where to aim.

This is the same contrastive multimodal-embedding idea you met in embedding models — and it powers image search too. In generation, the text encoder's output becomes the conditioning signal the denoiser reads at every step.

02  Cross-attention: the text → image bridge

So how do those text embeddings actually reach into the image? Through cross-attention — the exact mechanism from the attention page, used in its cross-modal form. The image patches form the Queries (“what should I become here?”), and the prompt tokens supply the Keys and Values (“here's what was asked for”). Each region of the forming image looks at the prompt and pulls in the words most relevant to it.

Cross-attention fires inside the denoiser at every step, so the prompt steers the whole reverse process. In MM-DiT this becomes a two-way joint stream (text reads image too) — see DiT & flow matching.

Pair this with the classifier-free guidance dial from the diffusion page and you have the two core text controls: cross-attention decides what the prompt says, guidance decides how forcefully to obey it.

03  ControlNet & adapters: control composition, not just content NEW

Text is great for content (“a knight on a horse”) but clumsy for composition (“…in exactly this pose, framed like this”). For spatial control you add a second conditioning channel:

ControlNet
A trainable copy of the denoiser's encoder that ingests a structural map — an edge/Canny outline, a human pose skeleton, a depth map, a segmentation — and injects it into the base model. The image follows that structure while text fills in style and detail. The base model stays frozen; you train only the add-on.
IP-Adapter
Feeds a reference image as conditioning (“make it look like this”) via an extra cross-attention path — image-prompting alongside text.
LoRA
Small low-rank adapters that teach a new style, character, or object cheaply, without retraining the whole model. The same PEFT idea used for LLMs, applied to image models.
Tracing paper over the canvas analogy

ControlNet is like laying tracing paper with a pose outline over the canvas before the artist paints. They're free to choose colors, lighting, and style (that's your text prompt), but they must keep the figure on the traced lines. You've separated composition (the outline) from content (the words) — the thing pure prompting can't reliably do.

04  img2img, inpainting & outpainting

You don't have to start from pure noise. Seed the process with an existing image and you get a family of edit tools:

img2img
Noise an input image only partway, then denoise with a new prompt. A strength knob sets how much to keep: low strength = gentle restyle, high strength = loosely inspired. Great for “same composition, new look.”
Inpainting
Re-generate only a masked region while the rest stays pixel-for-pixel fixed — remove an object, change a shirt color, fix a hand. The model fills the hole consistently with its surroundings and the prompt.
Outpainting
Inpainting's twin, pointed outward: extend the canvas beyond its edges, inventing plausible surroundings (zoom-out, widen a scene).

All three are the same denoiser — you're just choosing the starting point and which pixels are allowed to change.

05  Instruction-based editing NEW

The 2024–2026 leap in usability: edit an image by just telling it what to change — “make it night,” “remove the sunglasses,” “put her in a red coat” — with no mask and no strength slider. The model conditions on both your instruction and the original image, learning to apply the edit while leaving everything else intact.

Increasingly this is folded into multimodal models that handle understanding and generation together, so editing feels like a conversation (“now make the sky stormy”) and preserves identity across turns. It's the most natural control surface yet — plain language as the API.

“Edit” vs “regenerate” aren't the same gotcha

A plain text-to-image call makes a fresh image; an instruction edit must preserve the unmentioned parts of the original. If you describe it as “just prompt it again,” you'll miss the whole point — identity and untouched-region preservation are the hard, valuable part. Mention that consistency is what these models are specifically trained for.

07  Controlling composition, not just content

The question this whole page answers — “how do you control composition, not just content?” — has a layered answer worth delivering as a stack:

You want to control…Reach for…
Subject & style (content)Text prompt + guidance scale
Pose / layout / depth / edgesControlNet (structural maps)
Look of a reference imageIP-Adapter (image prompt)
A specific style / character / objectLoRA adapter
Keep a region, change the restimg2img / inpainting / outpainting
A targeted change in plain languageInstruction-based editing
How to say it out loud say it

“The prompt enters through cross-attention — image patches query CLIP-style text embeddings — and guidance scale sets how strongly it's obeyed. That controls content. For composition you add channels: ControlNet for pose/depth/edges, IP-Adapter for a reference image, LoRA for a style or character, and img2img/inpainting to keep some pixels and change others. Instruction editing wraps it all in plain language while preserving the parts you didn't mention.”

08  Self-check

You need the generated image to follow a specific human pose while the prompt sets the style. Best tool?
Pose is a structural/compositional constraint, which is exactly ControlNet's job — feed it a pose map and the image follows that geometry while the prompt fills in style. Guidance scale only strengthens text adherence (content), and the VAE affects detail/resolution, not layout.
“How does a text prompt actually influence the pixels?”Core
Model answer

The prompt is encoded by a CLIP-style text encoder into embeddings in a shared text–image space. Inside the denoiser, cross-attention lets each image patch (the Query) attend to those text embeddings (Keys/Values), pulling in the relevant words as it decides what to become. This happens at every denoising step, and classifier-free guidance amplifies the prompt's effect. In MM-DiT it's a joint, bidirectional stream so text and image inform each other.

How to say it out loud say it

“CLIP encodes the prompt; cross-attention lets image patches read those embeddings each step; guidance amplifies it. Words steer pixels through attention, not a lookup.”

“Inpainting vs img2img — what's the difference?”Warm-up
Model answer

img2img re-noises the whole image partway and denoises it under a new prompt, with a strength knob for how much to keep — it restyles everything a little. Inpainting locks all pixels except a masked region and regenerates only that hole to match the surroundings and prompt — surgical edits like removing an object. Same model; inpainting just constrains which pixels may change.

How to say it out loud say it

“img2img restyles the whole image by partial re-noising; inpainting regenerates only a masked region and freezes the rest. Both are the same denoiser with different start/lock choices.”
