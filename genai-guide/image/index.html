Image generation — overview IV

Text models predict the next word. Image models do something that sounds almost magical: they start from pure static — TV-snow noise — and carve a picture out of it, one denoising step at a time. This module unpacks how that works, why diffusion beat the older approaches, and how a few words of prompt steer the whole thing. Same big GenAI idea — learn the patterns, produce new examples — but with pixels instead of tokens.

01  The sculptor who removes noise

Here's the one mental model that makes every modern image generator click. Imagine a block of marble that's actually a block of random noise. A sculptor looks at it and asks, “if a cat were hiding in here, which specks are not cat?” — and chips a few away. The block looks slightly less noisy, slightly more cat-like. They repeat. After many passes, a clean image of a cat is standing where the noise used to be.

Michelangelo's marble analogy

Michelangelo supposedly said the statue was already inside the block — he just removed everything that wasn't the statue. A diffusion model works the same way: it starts from noise and, guided by your prompt, repeatedly removes “everything that isn't the picture.” The skill the model learned in training is simply knowing what counts as noise at each step.

That's the whole intuition for diffusion, the technique behind essentially every image generator you've used. The rest of this module makes it precise — but if you remember nothing else, remember the sculptor.

02  Why images are harder than text

A sentence is a short, ordered list of tokens from a fixed vocabulary — a few hundred to a few thousand symbols. An image is a million-plus pixels, each a continuous colour, all related to their neighbours at once. There's no natural “left to right” order, and “close enough” still has to look right to a human eye that's exquisitely good at spotting wrong hands and melted faces.

So image generation needs a method that (a) handles a huge continuous output, (b) captures global structure (a face) and fine texture (skin pores) together, and (c) gives smooth control over quality. Three big families tried; one clearly won.

03  The three families: GANs, VAEs, Diffusion NEW

Before diffusion took over (roughly 2021 onward), two other approaches dominated. You should be able to name all three and say crisply how they differ:

GAN
Generative Adversarial Network. Two networks duel: a generator fakes images, a discriminator tries to catch fakes. One shot, no iteration — fast, but notoriously unstable to train and prone to mode collapse (making the same few things).
VAE
Variational Autoencoder. Squeeze an image into a small latent code, then decode it back. Stable and gives a tidy latent space, but on its own produces blurry results.
Diffusion
Start from noise and iteratively denoise toward an image. Slower (many steps) but stable to train, mode-covering, and stunning in quality. The winner — and it cleverly reuses the VAE's latent space to stay efficient.

The key visual difference: a GAN guesses the whole image in one leap; diffusion takes many small, correctable steps — which is exactly why it's more stable and higher-quality.

04  Why diffusion won NEW

The honest interview answer isn't “it makes prettier pictures” — it's about training dynamics and coverage. Diffusion turns one impossibly hard problem (“draw a cat from nothing”) into thousands of easy ones (“remove a little noise”). Each tiny step is a stable regression problem, so training rarely diverges.

Diffusion's one real weakness — being slow because it takes many steps — is exactly what the rest of this module's research keeps attacking: latent space, better samplers, flow matching, and consistency models all chip away at the step count.

“GANs are dead” is too strong gotcha

Diffusion won for general text-to-image, but GANs aren't gone. Because they generate in a single pass, they're still used where you need real-time speed — super-resolution, face upscaling, some on-device effects. Say “diffusion dominates general image generation; GANs survive in latency-critical niches,” not “nobody uses GANs.”

How to say it out loud say it

“Image generation has had three big families: GANs, which pit a generator against a discriminator in one shot; VAEs, which encode to a latent and decode back; and diffusion, which starts from noise and iteratively denoises toward an image. Diffusion won general text-to-image because it's stable to train and covers the data distribution well — turning one hard problem into many easy denoising steps. Its only real cost is being slow, which is why the modern stack keeps reducing the number of steps.”

06  Self-check

An interviewer asks why diffusion replaced GANs for general text-to-image. What's the strongest answer?
Diffusion is actually slower than a one-shot GAN — that's its weakness, not its strength. It won on training stability and mode coverage: many small denoising regressions are far easier to optimize than a fragile adversarial game, and they avoid mode collapse. GANs can produce high-res images and still win where single-pass speed matters.
“Explain image generation to me like I'm ten.”Warm-up
Model answer

Imagine a screen full of TV static. The model has learned, from millions of pictures, what real images look like — so it can look at that static and slowly scrub away the parts that don't belong, a little at a time, until a clear picture is left. Your text prompt tells it what picture to aim for. It's a sculptor removing noise instead of marble.

How to say it out loud say it

“It starts from random noise and removes the bits that aren't the picture, step by step, guided by your prompt — like carving a statue out of static.”

“What is mode collapse, and which family suffers from it?”Core
Model answer

Mode collapse is when a generator learns to produce only a narrow slice of the possible outputs — say, the same handful of faces — because that reliably fools the discriminator. It's a classic GAN failure, born of the adversarial game. Diffusion and VAEs are trained to reconstruct the whole distribution, so they cover diversity much better.

How to say it out loud say it

“Mode collapse is a GAN producing only a few kinds of output to fool the discriminator. Diffusion avoids it because it's trained to reconstruct the full data distribution, not to win a game.”
