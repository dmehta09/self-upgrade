LLM internals · decoding

How text is generated IV

The model's final act each step is to hand you a score for every possible next token. Decoding is how we turn those scores into one actual word — and it's the only part of generation you control from an API. Temperature, top-p, top-k, penalties: master these and you can dial a model from rigidly factual to wildly creative.

01  Logits → softmax → a probability distribution

The last layer outputs one raw number per vocabulary token — these are logits. They're unbounded and not yet probabilities. To turn them into a clean distribution that sums to 1, we apply the softmax function, which exponentiates each logit and normalises. Bigger logit → bigger probability, and the gaps get exaggerated so the top candidates stand out.

From raw votes to percentages analogy

Logits are like raw applause levels for each candidate word — loud, quiet, somewhere between, on no fixed scale. Softmax is the host announcing “OK, that's 62% for ‘mat’, 25% for ‘rug’, 13% split among the rest.” Same ranking, now turned into shares that add up to 100%.

Next token after “The cat sat on the …”LogitProbability (softmax)
“mat”4.0~62%
“rug”3.1~25%
“floor”2.2~10%
“moon”0.1~1%

Illustrative numbers, not from a real model — but the shape is right: a few likely tokens carry most of the probability, with a long tail of unlikely ones.

02  Greedy vs sampling

Given that distribution, how do we pick? Two philosophies:

Greedy
Always take the single highest-probability token. Deterministic and “safe,” but can be repetitive and bland — and locally-best choices don't always make the best sentence.
Sampling
Draw a token at random according to the probabilities — “mat” 62% of the time, “rug” 25%, and so on. Introduces the variety and creativity people expect from a chatbot.

Almost all chat use is sampling, shaped by the knobs below. (A relative of greedy, beam search — keeping several candidate sequences alive — is common in translation but rare for open-ended chat, where it tends to sound flat.)

03  Temperature: the creativity dial

Temperature rescales the logits before softmax. Low temperature sharpens the distribution (the top token dominates → focused, repeatable); high temperature flattens it (long-tail tokens get a real chance → diverse, surprising, riskier). At temperature 0 you effectively get greedy decoding.

TemperatureEffect on the distributionUse it for
~0Spiky — top token nearly always winsExtraction, classification, code, anything factual
0.7–1.0BalancedGeneral chat, everyday writing
> 1.0Flat — rare tokens become likelyBrainstorming, creative variety (watch for incoherence)
Hot vs cold dice analogy

Low temperature is like loaded dice that almost always land on the favourite — predictable. High temperature is fair dice, or even dice tilted toward the underdogs — anything can come up. You're not changing what the model believes is likely, just how boldly it bets on the long shots.

04  Top-k, top-p (nucleus) & min-p

Temperature reshapes all the probabilities; these methods instead truncate the candidate set so you never sample absurd tokens, then sample from what's left:

Top-k
Keep only the k highest-probability tokens (e.g. k=40); ignore the rest. Simple, but a fixed k is too tight when many words are plausible and too loose when only one is.
Top-p (nucleus)
Keep the smallest set of tokens whose probabilities add up to p (e.g. p=0.9). Adapts automatically: a confident step keeps few tokens, an open-ended step keeps many. The most common choice.
Min-p
Keep tokens whose probability is at least a fraction of the top token's (e.g. min-p=0.05). Newer; stays sensible across temperatures by scaling the cutoff to the model's current confidence.

In practice you combine them: e.g. a moderate temperature plus top-p as a safety net. Lower the cutoffs for precise tasks, loosen them for creative ones.

05  Repetition, frequency & presence penalties

Left alone, sampling can loop (“…and then, and then, and then…”). Penalties discourage that by lowering the logits of tokens that have already appeared:

Frequency penalty
The more often a token has appeared, the more its score is reduced — scales with count. Curbs literal repetition.
Presence penalty
A flat reduction the moment a token has appeared at all, regardless of count. Nudges the model toward new topics and vocabulary.
Repetition penalty
A multiplicative cousin (common in open-source stacks) that divides the logits of seen tokens. Same goal, different math.

A light touch helps; crank them too high and the model avoids necessary words (it can't say “the” or a key term again) and quality drops.

06  Why temperature 0 isn't fully deterministic

A classic trap: people set temperature 0 expecting byte-identical outputs every time, then see drift. Temperature 0 makes the sampling deterministic (always take the argmax), but other things still wiggle the logits:

“Temp 0 = reproducible” is wrong on shared infra gotcha

Two things break it. Batching: your request is computed alongside others, and floating-point operations on GPUs aren't perfectly associative, so the exact logits shift slightly run-to-run — occasionally flipping a near-tie at the top. MoE routing: in a Mixture-of-Experts model, which experts a token hits can depend on its batch-mates, nudging the logits again. So temperature 0 gives you the most likely token, not a guaranteed identical transcript. For real reproducibility you also need a fixed seed and, ideally, dedicated/single-request execution.

07  Logprobs and what they're good for

Many APIs can return logprobs — the log-probability the model assigned to each token it chose (and often the top alternatives). They're a peek at the model's confidence, and they're genuinely useful:

Confidence & uncertainty
Low per-token logprobs flag shaky spans — handy for hallucination detection or routing low-confidence answers to review.
Cheap classification
For a yes/no or multiple-choice prompt, compare the logprobs of “Yes” vs “No” instead of parsing free text — fast and robust.
Constrained scoring
Rank candidate answers by total logprob; used in some eval and retrieval-rerank setups.
logprob_to_prob.py
import math

# A token's logprob is ln(probability). Convert back with exp:
logprob = -0.22
probability = math.exp(logprob)   # about 0.80, i.e. the model was ~80% sure
print(round(probability, 3))
How to say it out loud say it

“The model outputs logits; softmax turns them into a probability distribution, and we sample from it. Temperature rescales the logits — low is focused, high is creative — while top-p and top-k truncate the candidate set so we never pick absurd tokens. Penalties reduce repetition. And temperature 0 isn't fully deterministic on shared infrastructure, because batching's floating-point non-determinism and MoE routing can nudge the logits.”

08  Self-check

How does top-p (nucleus) sampling differ from top-k?
Top-k always keeps exactly k candidates; top-p keeps a variable number — few when the model is confident, many when it isn't — by accumulating probability up to p. That adaptiveness is why top-p is the more common default.
“A user complains the model isn't reproducible at temperature 0. What do you tell them?”Core
Model answer

Temperature 0 only makes the choice deterministic — always the argmax. The logits themselves can still shift slightly between runs because GPU floating-point ops aren't associative and your request is batched with others, and because MoE expert routing can depend on batch-mates. Those tiny shifts can flip a near-tie at the top. For tighter reproducibility, fix a seed, reduce batching/use dedicated capacity if available, and accept that exact determinism isn't guaranteed on shared inference.

How to say it out loud say it

“Temp 0 fixes the sampling, not the logits. Batching's floating-point non-determinism and MoE routing can nudge the scores and flip a close call — so it's ‘most likely,’ not ‘byte-identical.’”

“When would you turn temperature down vs up?”Warm-up
Model answer

Down (near 0) for tasks with a “correct” answer: extraction, classification, code, structured output, factual Q&A — you want focus and repeatability. Up (~0.8–1.2) for open-ended creativity: brainstorming, varied copy, dialogue — you want diversity, accepting more risk of incoherence. I'd usually pair a moderate temperature with top-p as a guardrail rather than pushing temperature very high alone.

How to say it out loud say it

“Low temperature for correctness — extraction, code, classification; higher for creativity — brainstorming, varied writing. I lean on top-p as a safety net rather than extreme temperatures.”
