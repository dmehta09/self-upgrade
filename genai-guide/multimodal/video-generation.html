Audio, video & VLMs · video generation

Video generation IV NEW

If image generation is painting a single picture from noise, video generation is painting a whole flipbook at once — and every page has to agree with the ones before and after it. It's the same diffusion idea you met for images, stretched across time as well as space. The 2026 leap: the best systems now generate the matching sound at the same time.

01  Diffusion, but across time too

Recall how image diffusion works: start from pure noise and repeatedly denoise it into a coherent picture, guided by your prompt. Video does the same — but instead of denoising one frame, it denoises many frames jointly, so they form a smooth, consistent motion rather than a flickering slideshow of unrelated pictures.

Carving a flipbook from fog analogy

An image model carves one statue out of a block of fog. A video model carves a whole flipbook out of a fog brick — all the pages at once — and as it sharpens them it keeps insisting that page 12 follows naturally from page 11. The hard part isn't drawing any single frame; it's making the dog on page 1 still be the same dog, running the same direction, on page 60.

02  Spatiotemporal latent grids + DiT denoisers NEW

Doing this pixel-by-pixel would be impossibly expensive, so video models work in a compressed latent space — and they compress in time as well as space. The clip becomes a 3-D grid of latent tokens: width × height × time. A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) then denoises that whole grid, using attention to keep every patch consistent with its neighbours both within a frame and across frames.

“Spatiotemporal” just means the model treats time as another axis to attend over. Temporal attention is what stops frame 2 from forgetting what was in frame 1 — it's the source of both the magic and the cost.

This is the same DiT + flow-matching machinery from the image module, with one extra dimension — see DiT & flow matching for the 2-D version. Add the time axis and you've got video.

03  Native synchronized audio — the 2026 leap NEW

Until recently, generated video was silent — you bolted on sound afterwards, and lip-sync and footsteps never quite matched. The defining advance of 2026 is native synchronized audio: the model generates the picture and the matching soundtrack together, from the same prompt, so dialogue lands on the right lip movements and the door-slam hits exactly when the door shuts.

“Has sound” ≠ “generated the sound with the video” gotcha

Plenty of tools play audio over a clip by running a separate sound model afterward. That's not the same as native generation, where one model produces audio and video jointly so they're inherently in sync. In an interview, draw the distinction: post-hoc dubbing vs joint audio-visual generation. The latter is what makes 2026 clips feel real.

04  Conditioning: text, image & multi-clip control NEW

“Conditioning” is just what you give the model to steer the output. Video supports several handles, and naming them shows range:

Text-to-video
A prompt describes the scene and the model invents everything. Maximum freedom, minimum control.
Image-to-video
You supply a starting frame (a real photo or a generated image) and the model animates from it. Great for art-directing the look before motion exists.
Multi-clip / keyframe control
Pin specific frames or stitch shots together — set the first and last frame, or chain clips so a character and setting persist across a longer sequence. This is how creators fight the consistency problem.
Other signals
Camera-motion prompts, depth or pose guidance, reference video for style — the same adapter/ControlNet spirit from images, extended to motion.

Specific products churn fast, so reason about capabilities, not version numbers. At a concept level, the leading families share the spatiotemporal-DiT recipe and increasingly offer native audio:

06  The hard, unsolved parts

Video generation is the least “solved” modality in this guide. Knowing the open problems is what separates hype from understanding:

Consistency
Keeping a face, object or background identical across frames (and across cuts). Faces morph, objects pop in and out, text on signs scrambles.
Length
Coherence degrades as clips get longer; most strong output is still short (seconds). Long video usually means stitching clips, which strains consistency.
Controllability
Precise control — “move the camera left, then have her wave” — is still coarse compared to the fine control image tools enjoy.
Cost & latency
That 3-D latent grid is huge, so generation is slow and expensive — far from real-time. Physics glitches (objects passing through each other) also persist.

07  How video gen differs from image gen

Tie it together. Video isn't “image generation, but more” — the time axis changes the problem qualitatively:

How to say it out loud say it

“Video generation is diffusion extended over time. The clip is compressed into a 3-D spatiotemporal latent grid — space plus time — and a Diffusion Transformer denoises the whole grid, using temporal attention so frames stay consistent. The 2026 leap is native synchronized audio: picture and sound generated jointly so they're in sync. It conditions on text, a start image, or keyframes. The hard parts are consistency, length, controllability and cost — it's the least solved modality.”

08  Self-check

What does the “temporal” part of a spatiotemporal latent grid buy a video model?
Treating time as an extra axis lets the DiT attend across frames, keeping objects, identities and motion coherent over the clip. Without temporal modelling you'd get a slideshow of plausible-but-disconnected images.
“How is video generation different from image generation?”Core
Model answer

Same diffusion foundation, but the latent is 3-D — space plus time — and the denoiser uses temporal attention so frames stay consistent rather than flickering. It must satisfy two demands at once: each frame looks right and the motion is coherent. The 2026 systems also generate synchronized audio jointly. All of that makes it far slower and pricier, and consistency over length is still the central unsolved problem.

How to say it out loud say it

“Add a time axis. The latent goes 3-D, the DiT adds temporal attention for consistency, and 2026 models generate synced audio too — at much higher cost. Consistency over length is the hard part.”
