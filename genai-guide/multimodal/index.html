Audio, video & VLMs · overview

Multimodal — beyond text NEW

So far this guide has lived in the world of words. But the same engine that predicts the next token can be pointed at pixels, soundwaves and frames. Multimodal simply means a model that works in more than one kind of data at once — reading an image, hearing a voice, watching a clip, or generating any of them. This module gives our text models eyes, ears, and a movie camera, and ends at the frontier where the lines between them blur into one any-to-any model.

01  Giving the model senses

A language model on its own is like a brilliant pen-pal locked in a windowless room: it has read everything ever written, but it has never seen a sunset, heard a song, or watched anything move. Multimodal is the work of cutting windows into that room. Each new modality is a new sense bolted onto the same underlying brain.

Eyes, ears and a movie camera analogy

Think of the text LLM as a mind that can only read and write notes. This module hands it three new organs: eyes (vision-language models that can look at a photo or document), ears (speech models that listen and talk back), and a movie camera (video generators that imagine moving scenes — now with sound). Same mind underneath; far more of the world reaches it.

The trick that makes this possible is one you've already met: every kind of data — words, image patches, audio snippets — gets turned into the same currency, a sequence of vectors the transformer can chew on. Once a picture becomes “tokens,” attention treats it much like text.

02  Two directions: understanding vs generation

Every modality splits into two jobs, and interviewers love it when you separate them cleanly:

Understanding (in)
The model consumes non-text and reasons about it — describe this photo, transcribe this call, answer questions about this PDF. The output is usually text.
Generation (out)
The model produces non-text — speak this sentence aloud, draw this scene, render this video. The input is usually text (a prompt).

A photo-captioner is understanding; a text-to-speech voice is generation. The most capable 2026 systems do both at once — you talk to them and they talk back, you show them a chart and they redraw it — but the two directions still use different machinery, so it pays to name which one you mean.

03  The convergence toward any-to-any NEW

The big story of 2025–2026 isn't any single modality — it's that they're merging. Early systems were a zoo of specialists glued together: a separate transcriber, a separate image model, a separate text brain, passed along a pipeline. The frontier today is one any-to-any model with a shared core that takes any mix of text, image, audio and video in, and emits any mix out.

Unifying the modalities cuts latency (no hand-offs), preserves nuance (tone, timing, layout survive instead of being flattened to text), and lets one model reason across senses — watch a video and hear it at once. The frontier page revisits this as “world models” (as of June 2026).

Why does this matter for an interview? Because it reframes the whole field: instead of “the speech model” and “the vision model,” you talk about one foundation model with multiple input and output heads. That single idea ties this entire module together.

“Multimodal” ≠ “generates everything” gotcha

A model that can read images and audio is multimodal even if it only writes text back. Most production “multimodal LLMs” are like this — strong on the understanding side, text-only on the output side. Don't assume a model that captions photos can also draw them; check which directions and which modalities it actually supports.

04  Why this works at all

Three ideas, all of which you've already seen elsewhere in the guide, make multimodality possible:

  1. Everything becomes tokens. An image is cut into patches, audio into short frames; each is embedded into the same vector space the transformer uses for words. The model doesn't care whether a vector came from a noun or a pixel-patch.
  2. Shared embedding spaces. Contrastive training (CLIP-style) teaches a model to place a picture of a dog and the word “dog” near each other, so text and images speak a common language — the backbone of both vision-language models and image generators.
  3. Diffusion generalises across dimensions. The same denoising idea that paints images extends to time, which is how video generation works.

If those phrases feel shaky, the Embeddings and Diffusion pages are the prerequisites — this module leans on both.

06  Self-check

A teammate says “our chatbot reads uploaded screenshots, so it must be able to generate images too.” What's the cleanest correction?
Understanding (image in → text out) and generation (text in → image out) use different machinery. Plenty of multimodal LLMs are vision-understanding only. Always check which directions and modalities a given model actually supports.
“What does ‘multimodal’ actually mean?”Warm-up
Model answer

A multimodal model works in more than one type of data — text, images, audio, video — rather than text alone. Under the hood each modality is turned into vectors in a shared space so the same transformer can process them. It splits into understanding (consuming non-text) and generation (producing it), and the 2026 frontier is unifying both into single any-to-any models.

How to say it out loud say it

“Multimodal means more than one kind of data. Everything gets embedded into a shared vector space, so one transformer can read images and audio, not just text. Separate the understanding direction from the generation direction — and note that the frontier is merging them into one any-to-any model.”

Follow-ups: Name a vision understanding vs a vision generation task. · Why turn an image into tokens at all?
“Why are the modalities converging into one model?”Core
Model answer

Because gluing specialists into a pipeline loses information and adds latency at every hand-off — transcribing speech to text throws away tone and timing before the LLM ever sees it. A single shared core keeps the nuance, removes the hops, and can reason across senses at once. That's why frontier systems are any-to-any rather than a chain of single-purpose models.

How to say it out loud say it

“Pipelines flatten nuance and add latency at each hand-off. A unified core keeps tone, timing and layout, cuts the hops, and reasons across modalities — that's the pull toward any-to-any models.”
