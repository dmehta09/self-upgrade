Organizations, SCPs & Identity Center.

Multi-account is the Sep 2026 default: isolate prod, apply SCPs as guardrails, and sign humans in with IAM Identity Center (SSO) — not shared IAM users or long-lived keys.

01 Organizations & account strategy

AWS Organizations groups accounts under one bill and one policy tree. Typical OUs: Security, Infrastructure, Sandbox, Workloads (dev/test/prod). One app blast radius ≠ one huge account.

In plain English analogy

Think of a company campus: each building (account) has its own locks. The corporate policy office posts rules on every door (SCPs) — “no petrol in the library.” Security badges for people come from one HQ desk (Identity Center), not a unique metal key cut per building.

02 SCPs: guardrails, not grants

SCPs never grant permissions — they set the maximum available in an account. IAM still must Allow. FullAWSAccess is the default; tighten with Deny statements (regions, leave-org, disable CloudTrail, root actions).

deny-unapproved-regions.json
{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [{
    "Sid": "DenyOutsideEU",
    "Effect": "Deny",
    "NotAction": ["cloudfront:*", "iam:*", "organizations:*", "route53:*", "support:*"],
    "Resource": "*",
    "Condition": {
      "StringNotEquals": {
        "aws:RequestedRegion": ["eu-west-1", "eu-central-1"]
      }
    }
  }]
}
Common trap gotcha

SCPs do not apply to the management account the same way teams expect for day-to-day lockdown — and an SCP Deny can break global services if you forget exceptions (IAM, Route 53, CloudFront, Support). Always allowlist those when region-fencing.

03 IAM Identity Center (SSO)

Identity Center is how humans get into AWS in 2026: workforce identity → permission sets → accounts. Temporary credentials via AWS access portal; optional ABAC with attributes.

Identity Center
  • One portal for many accounts
  • Permission sets ≈ managed role templates
  • Integrates with Okta / Entra ID / Google
  • Replaces per-account IAM users for people
Anti-pattern
  • Shared “admin” IAM user + access keys
  • Long-lived keys on laptops
  • Direct IAM users in every account
  • Root user for daily work

Workloads still use IAM roles (instance profiles, OIDC) — Identity Center is for people. Role mechanics: IAM users, roles & policies.

A company wants developers to access three AWS accounts with MFA and no long-lived keys. Best approach?

Right. Identity Center + permission sets is the multi-account human access pattern. SCPs don’t grant access; root and long-lived keys are wrong.

04 Interview rapid-fire

What do SCPs actually do?core
Model answer

SCPs are organization guardrails that define the ceiling of permissions in member accounts. They don’t grant anything by themselves — IAM identity and resource policies still must Allow. Use them to deny dangerous actions or unapproved regions across OUs.

Say it out loud say it

"SCPs are fences, not keys — they cap what an account can ever do. IAM still has to grant the action inside that fence."

Identity Center vs IAM users for employees?core
Model answer

Use Identity Center for workforce access across accounts with permission sets and short-lived credentials. IAM users with access keys are for exceptional break-glass cases, not daily human access in a modern multi-account setup.

Say it out loud say it

"People get Identity Center permission sets — temporary creds into each account. Workloads get roles. No long-lived human access keys."

Why multi-account instead of one big account?warm-up
Model answer

Blast-radius isolation, clearer billing, different SCP guardrails per OU, and cleaner least privilege. Prod compromise shouldn’t equal sandbox compromise. Centralize logging in Security OU accounts.

Say it out loud say it

"Multi-account isolates blast radius and lets SCPs differ by OU — prod fences stay tighter than sandbox."
