Foundations · exam method

How to take SAA-C03 without drowning.

SAA-C03 is a timed scenario exam: eliminate wrong architectures, don’t invent services. As of 2026 the four domains still drive scoring — Secure, Resilient, High-performing, Cost-optimized. Method beats memorizing every feature flag.

01 Know what you’re graded on

Every question maps to at least one domain. Train your eye to spot domain keywords before you fall in love with a service name.

In plain English analogy

Think of the exam as four different judges scoring the same blueprint. One cares about locks, one about earthquakes, one about elevator speed, one about the construction budget. The winning design is the one that satisfies the judge who’s speaking in that question — not the one that impresses all four maximally.

02 Elimination technique

Most stems have one hard constraint that kills 2–3 options. Circle it. Then pick the simplest remaining AWS-native design.

1

Underline constraints

Must, cannot, RPO/RTO, “private only,” “global users,” “least operational overhead.”

2

Kill anti-patterns

Public RDS, long-lived keys on EC2, single-AZ HA claims, NAT for S3 when endpoints exist.

3

Prefer managed + Multi-AZ

Unless the stem forces OS control or multi-Region.

4

Flag and move

~1.5–2 minutes average. Mark tough scenario sets; return with fresh eyes.

Common trap gotcha

Answer shopping by brand familiarity. “I’ve used EC2” doesn’t beat Fargate/Lambda when the stem wants least ops. “I’ve heard of Transit Gateway” doesn’t beat VPC peering/PrivateLink when the topology is simpler. Pick the service that matches the constraint, not your résumé.

03 What to memorize vs reason

Memorize sharp dichotomies (SG vs NACL, gateway vs interface endpoint, ALB vs NLB). Reason about scenarios using Well-Architected trade-offs.

Memorize
  • SG stateful allow-only vs NACL stateless
  • S3/DynamoDB gateway endpoints are free of hourly ENI charges
  • RDS Multi-AZ = sync standby; read replica = scaling/DR aid
  • CloudFront origins, OAC/OAI for S3
Reason
  • Multi-AZ vs multi-Region from RPO/RTO
  • SQL vs Dynamo from access pattern
  • When Spot is safe (stateless, fault-tolerant)
  • When to add caching vs scale vertically

A question says “most cost-effective” and “highly available within one Region.” One option is multi-Region active-active; another is Multi-AZ with an ALB. Which wins?

Right. “Within one Region” + cost → Multi-AZ. Multi-Region fails the cost/scope test. Single AZ is not HA.

04 Exam rapid-fire

What’s your first 10 seconds on a hard SAA question?warm-up
Model answer

Find the hard constraint and the domain keyword. Mentally discard anything that violates them before comparing the remaining two options on cost or ops overhead.

Say it out loud say it

"Constraint first, eliminate violators, then pick the simplest managed option left."

Name two classic wrong answers you’ll auto-reject.core
Model answer

Publicly reachable databases, and “HA” designs stuck in a single AZ. Also: storing IAM secrets on instances when roles exist; using NAT for heavy S3 when a gateway endpoint fits.

Say it out loud say it

"I instantly reject public DBs and single-AZ HA claims."

How do you handle “least operational overhead”?stretch
Model answer

Prefer fully managed and serverless options that remove OS/patching/HA scripting — Aurora/DynamoDB/Fargate/Lambda/S3 — unless a hard constraint blocks them.

Say it out loud say it

"Least ops means managed or serverless — I only pick EC2 DIY when the stem forces it."
