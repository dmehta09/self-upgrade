Foundations · Regions & AZs Domain 2 · Resilient

Region, AZ, edge — pick the blast radius.

Availability Zones are the unit of high availability inside a Region. Regions are the unit of disaster recovery and data residency. Edge locations shrink latency for users far from your Region — they don’t replace Multi-AZ for your stateful tier.

01 The location hierarchy

A Region is a geographic area with multiple isolated Availability Zones (data centers with independent power/networking). Edge locations / Points of Presence sit closer to users for CDN and DNS.

In plain English analogy

A Region is a city. Each AZ is a separate power grid and water main in that city — a fire in one neighborhood doesn’t black out the others. Edge locations are newsstands in every town: they cache the paper so locals don’t drive to the city for every read.

flowchart TD
  region[Region] --> aza[AZ_a]
  region --> azb[AZ_b]
  region --> azc[AZ_c]
  edge[Edge_PoPs] -.cache_DNS.-> users[Users]
  users --> region
Region
Choose for latency to users, compliance (data residency), and service availability. Cross-Region is deliberate DR, not default HA.
Availability Zone
One or more discrete data centers. Subnets are pinned to a single AZ. Spread stateful and compute tiers across ≥2 AZs.
Local Zones / Wavelength
Extend select services closer for ultra-low latency (gaming, 5G). Exam rare — know they exist for “single-digit ms to metro.”
Edge
CloudFront, Route 53, Global Accelerator — improve reach without moving your primary Region.

02 Multi-AZ vs multi-Region

Multi-AZ survives an AZ failure (typical HA). Multi-Region survives a Region event and meets residency/latency needs — higher cost and complexity.

GoalPatternExam signal
Survive one AZ outageMulti-AZ ASG, ALB, RDS Multi-AZ, Aurora“highly available within a Region”
Survive Region loss / low RPO across geographyPilot light / warm standby / active-active, S3 CRR, Aurora Global, DynamoDB global tables“disaster recovery,” “another Region”
Faster global readsCloudFront, Route 53 latency routing, read replicas“users worldwide,” “lowest latency”
Common trap gotcha

Two instances in one AZ is not HA. An AZ event takes both. Always ask: “Are resources in at least two AZs behind a Multi-AZ-aware control plane?” Same trap: using CloudFront alone and calling the origin “highly available” when the origin is single-AZ.

03 Modern defaults (2026)

For stateful data in one Region: Multi-AZ by default. For static/global content: S3 + CloudFront. For DNS failover: Route 53 health checks only when you truly have a secondary.

Under-scoped
  • Single-AZ RDS “to save money”
  • ASG min=2, all in us-east-1a
  • Assuming edge = Region failover
Exam-aligned
  • RDS/Aurora Multi-AZ or Aurora Serverless v2 across AZs
  • ASG / Fargate tasks across subnets in ≥2 AZs
  • Edge for performance; Multi-Region only for DR/residency

Users are global; the app must stay up if one AZ fails, but RPO of hours for a full Region loss is OK. What’s the lean design?

Right. AZ failure → Multi-AZ. Global users → edge/CDN. Soft Region RPO → don’t pay for active-active unless required. Single AZ + CDN still dies when that AZ dies.

04 Exam rapid-fire

Why can’t a subnet span two AZs?warm-up
Model answer

A subnet is bound to exactly one AZ. HA means creating subnets in each AZ and placing resources (or letting ASG/RDS place them) across those subnets.

Say it out loud say it

"One subnet, one AZ — so for HA I create matching subnets in two AZs and spread the workload."

When do you choose multi-Region over Multi-AZ?core
Model answer

When the business needs survival past a Region event, data residency in multiple geographies, or very low latency writes in distant markets. Otherwise Multi-AZ in one Region is the cost-effective HA default.

Say it out loud say it

"Multi-AZ for AZ loss; multi-Region only when RPO/RTO or residency demands surviving a whole Region."

Does CloudFront make your origin Multi-AZ?stretch
Model answer

No. CloudFront caches and absorbs edge traffic, but origin failures still need Multi-AZ (or multi-origin failover). Edge ≠ AZ redundancy for dynamic, uncached requests.

Say it out loud say it

"CDN helps users and absorbs load; the origin still needs Multi-AZ if uncached traffic must survive an AZ outage."
