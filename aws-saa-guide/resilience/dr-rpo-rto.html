Resilience & DR · RPO & RTO Domain 2 · Resilient

DR is a budget, not a checkbox.

RPO is how much data you can lose. RTO is how long you can be down. Every DR strategy on SAA-C03 is really "how warm is the standby?" — and warmer always costs more.

01 RPO vs RTO

Business sets the targets; architecture spends money to meet them. Lower numbers → continuous replication, warm/hot standbys, multi-Region.

In plain English analogy

RPO is how far back your undo button goes (last backup vs live mirror). RTO is how long until the store reopens after a fire — empty lot vs fully staffed second store across town.

RPO — data
  • "Lose at most 5 minutes of writes"
  • Drives backup / replication frequency
  • Near-zero → sync or continuous replication
RTO — time
  • "Be serving again within 1 hour"
  • Drives standby warmth + runbooks
  • Near-zero → hot / active-active

02 The four DR strategies

From cheapest/slowest to most expensive/fastest. Match the stem's RPO/RTO language to a tier.

  warm --> multi[Multi_Site_Active]

03 AWS building blocks

AWS Backup
Central policies across EBS, RDS, DynamoDB, EFS, etc. Still restore-test.
RDS / Aurora
Automated backups + PITR (RPO); Multi-AZ (RTO in-Region); cross-Region replica / Aurora Global (Region DR).
S3
Versioning + CRR; Object Lock for immutability when ransomware is in scope.
Route 53
Failover / health-check routing to the DR stack.
AMI / Launch templates
Pilot light / warm standby need known-good images ready to scale.
Common trap gotcha

An untested backup is not a DR plan. Stems that emphasize meeting a stated RTO want warm/hot capacity — not "we'll restore from snapshot someday." Also don't confuse Multi-AZ HA with Region DR.

Requirement: RTO under 15 minutes, RPO under 5 minutes, tolerate Region failure, minimize idle cost. Best fit?

Right. Tight RTO/RPO across Regions needs replicated data plus some always-on capacity — not cold daily backups, and not full active-active unless cost is no object.

04 Exam rapid-fire

Define RPO and RTO in one breath.warm-up
Model answer

RPO = maximum acceptable data loss (time). RTO = maximum acceptable downtime. Architecture and spend follow those two numbers.

Say it out loud say it

"RPO is data; RTO is clocks. Tighter either way costs more."

Pilot light vs warm standby?core
Model answer

Pilot light keeps the data plane (and tiny core) on; you launch/scale most compute at failover. Warm standby runs a scaled-down but complete stack always — faster RTO, higher idle cost.

Say it out loud say it

"Pilot light is a spark; warm standby is a dimmed full house. Both beat cold restore when minutes matter."

When is active-active justified?stretch
Model answer

When RTO/RPO are near-zero and the business accepts ~2× capacity plus multi-Region data design (Global Tables, Aurora Global, conflict rules). Don't pick it just because "HA" appears in the stem.

Say it out loud say it

"Active-active is the luxury tier — buy it for zero-downtime Region failure, not for routine AZ loss."
