How aptitude tests work.

Before any shortcut, know the battlefield. The online assessment (OA) that gates a tech interview isn't a maths exam — it's a speed-and-triage game. Win it by playing the format, not just the questions.

01 Anatomy of a placement OA

A typical OA bundles four sections — Quantitative Aptitude, Logical Reasoning, Verbal Ability, Data Interpretation — under a hard clock, with roughly 30–60 seconds per question.

You almost never finish everything. The score that clears the cut-off comes from grabbing the easy marks fast and refusing to bleed time on the hard ones.

In plain English analogy

Think of it as an ER triage, not a final exam. A flood of patients arrives and the clock is brutal — you treat the quick, walk-in cases first and stabilise as many as possible. The one bleeding case that needs an hour? It waits. Marks, like patients, are saved by speed and order, not heroics.

Numbers vary by company, but the shape holds. Three twists change how you should play:

Sectional cut-offs
Each section has its own pass mark — acing Quant can't rescue a failed Verbal. You must clear every section, so don't starve your weakest one of time.
Adaptive vs fixed
Some tests (e.g. TCS NQT) are adaptive — answer right and the next question gets harder; many others are fixed. In adaptive mode you usually can't revisit a question, so commit.
Negative marking
Some OAs deduct for wrong answers (often −¼ or −⅓). This single rule flips your guessing strategy completely — check it before you start.

02 The universal solve method

Every question, every section: spot the type → recall the shortcut → eliminate options → commit (guess smart or skip). Same four beats, on repeat.

1

Spot the type

Read the first line and name it: "this is successive-percentage", "this is a blood-relation tree". Recognition is 80% of speed — you've trained the pattern, so trust it.

2

Recall the shortcut

Each type has a trick — a fraction, a multiplier, a diagram. Reach for it instead of grinding algebra. No shortcut in 10 seconds? Flag and move on.

3

Eliminate options

It's MCQ — kill the absurd answers first. Wrong sign, wrong order of magnitude, wrong unit. Two left is a coin-flip; one left is a free mark.

4

Commit: guess or skip

No negative marking → never leave a blank; guess from the survivors. Negative marking on → only answer if you've eliminated to 2 or fewer; otherwise skip.

Common trap gotcha

Sinking 5 minutes into one stubborn question to "get your money's worth" is the classic score-killer. At ~40s a question, those 5 minutes were 8 easy marks you'll now never reach. One hard question and one easy question are worth the same — so always buy the cheap one first.

03 Time triage: the 90-second rule

Do two passes. Pass 1: sweep the section and answer only what falls in under ~40s. Pass 2: return to the flagged ones with whatever time is left.

Cap any single question at about 90 seconds. Hit the cap with no clear path? Eliminate what you can, mark it, and leave — momentum on easy questions beats stubbornness on one.

Pro tip tip

On a sectional-cut-off test, watch the clock per section, not just overall. Leaving Verbal with 8 minutes because Quant ran long can fail you on Verbal even with a great total.

A fixed (non-adaptive) test has no negative marking and 30 seconds left with 6 questions blank. Best move?

Fill every blank. With no penalty, a blank scores 0 but a guess has a ~25% shot — strictly better. The trap is treating a guess as risky when the rules make it free expected value.

04 Play it fast

Negative marking is ON (−¼ per wrong). You've cut a question down to 2 plausible options. Guess or skip?warm-up
Decide it

Down to 2, your guess wins half the time. Expected value = ½(+1) + ½(−¼) = +⅜ per such guess — positive, so guess.

The fast way shortcut

Rule of thumb with −¼ marking: eliminated to 2 or fewer → guess; can't eliminate anything (blind 1-in-4) → skip. Don't compute EV in the exam — just remember "two or fewer, fire away."

You're 90 seconds into one question and still stuck. What do you do?core
Decide it

Stop. Eliminate any clearly-wrong options, then flag it and move on — guess now if there's no penalty, revisit on pass 2 if time allows. The answer is leave it.

The fast way shortcut

Set a mental 90s ceiling per question. Crossing it means the expected cost of staying (lost easy marks) now beats the gain. Bank the easy ones first; heroics come last, if at all.

The test says "each section has a cut-off." What does a sectional cut-off mean for your plan?stretch
Decide it

You must independently clear every section's minimum — a strong total can't cover a weak section. So budget time per section and never let your weakest one run dry.

The fast way shortcut

Cut-offs make every section pass/fail. Hit a safe margin in each first, then spend leftover time chasing your strongest section for the overall score. Survive all four before maximising one.
