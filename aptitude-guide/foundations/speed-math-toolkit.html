Test Strategy & Speed · speed math

The speed-math toolkit.

Under a 40-second clock, long multiplication loses. This is the mental-math kit the toppers run on autopilot — a handful of tricks that turn ugly arithmetic into one clean step.

01 Why tricks beat arithmetic

Almost every "scary" calculation hides an easy reshape: ×5 is really ÷2, ×25 is really ÷4, 12.5% is really ÷8. Learn the reshape and you skip the grind.

You won't compute faster than the clock — you'll compute less. The goal isn't speed-arithmetic; it's spotting the shortcut so there's barely any arithmetic left.

In plain English analogy

These tricks are keyboard shortcuts. A beginner hunts through menus — File, Save As, click, confirm — while a pro just taps Ctrl+S. Same result, a tenth of the time. Long multiplication is the menu; ×5 = ÷2 then ×10 is the shortcut key.

02 The multiplication tricks

Four reshapes cover most of what the exam throws at you. Each replaces a multiplication with a halving, a quartering, or a tiny addition.

×5 = ÷2 then ×10 # 86 × 5 → 43 → 430
×25 = ÷4 then ×100 # 36 × 25 → 9 → 900
×11 = write the digit, then each neighbour-sum, then the last digit # 35 × 11 → 3 (3+5) 5 → 385
n5² = n×(n+1) then stick on 25 # 35² → 3×4=12 → 1225

Slow way · 24 × 25

Stack it: 24 × 25 = 24 × 20 + 24 × 5 = 480 + 120 = 600. Three lines, two chances to slip.

Fast way · 24 × 25

×25 = ÷4 then ×100. 24 ÷ 4 = 6 → 600. One step, done in your head.

Heads up note

For ×11 when a neighbour-sum exceeds 9, carry the 1 left. 57 × 11: 5 (5+7=12) 7 → 5 (carry) → 627.

03 %↔fraction table + approximation + unit digits

Memorise the percent-to-fraction map cold — a percentage problem becomes a single division. Then approximate to land the order of magnitude, and use unit-digit cycles when only the last digit matters.

Common trap gotcha

Approximation only works when the options are far apart. If the choices are 818, 824 and 831, rounding both numbers can push you to the wrong neighbour. When options cluster, don't over-round — compute exactly, or round only one side and adjust.

Fastest way to compute 12.5% of 256?

256 ÷ 8 = 32. All three reach 32, but the fraction route is one division. The "× 12.5 then ÷ 100" path is exactly the long multiplication the trick exists to avoid.

04 Back-solve from the options

It's MCQ — the answer is already on the screen. When the algebra is slow, plug the options in and see which one fits. Start from the middle choice to halve your tries.

Pair this with elimination: kill any option with the wrong sign, wrong unit, or wrong last digit, then test only the survivors.

Pro tip tip

"A number ×1.25 = 75" — instead of solving, test the round option: 60 × 1.25 = 75. Match on the first try. Back-solving turns "find x" into "check x", which is far faster.

05 Solve it fast

Compute 25 × 16 quickly.warm-up
Solve it

×25 = ÷4 then ×100. 16 ÷ 4 = 4 → 400.

The fast way shortcut

Never long-multiply by 25 — it's a quarter of 100. Divide by 4 and tack on two zeros. Same for ×50 (÷2, ×100) and ×125 (÷8, ×1000).

Find 45².core
Solve it

Number ends in 5, so n = 4: n×(n+1) = 4 × 5 = 20, then stick on 25 → 2025.

The fast way shortcut

Any number ending in 5: chop the 5, multiply the front by the next integer, append 25. 75² → 7×8 = 56 → 5625. 95² → 9×10 = 90 → 9025.

What is the unit (last) digit of 7⁴?stretch
Solve it

7's last digit cycles every 4 powers: 7, 9, 3, 1. The 4th power lands on 1.

The fast way shortcut

For "last digit of a power", never compute the full number. Find the cycle length (here 4), take the exponent mod 4, and read off the cycle. Exponent ÷ 4 leaves remainder 0 → the 4th slot → 1.
