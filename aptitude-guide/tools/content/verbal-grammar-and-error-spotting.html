Grammar & error spotting.

"Spot the error" lives off a handful of rules tested again and again. Learn the seven high-frequency traps and you'll catch most of them on sight.

01 The idea

Error-spotting is pattern recognition, not deep grammar. The same few rules — subject–verb agreement, tense, pronouns, prepositions, comparatives — supply nearly every question. Check the verb against its real subject first.

Don't read for "what sounds off". Run a fixed checklist over each underlined part: does the verb match the subject, is the tense consistent, does each pronoun have a clear singular/plural noun?

In plain English analogy

Think of it like a code review for a sentence. You don't re-read the whole file emotionally — you run a linter that checks the same recurring bugs. Subject–verb mismatch is the "undefined variable" of English: catch it first, every time.

02 The seven high-frequency errors

This table is the syllabus. Scan each underlined chunk for these, in roughly this order.

Error typeWrongRight
Subject–verb agreementThe list of items are long.The list of items is long. (subject = list)
Tense consistencyHe came in and sits down.He came in and sat down.
Pronoun agreementEach student lost their book.Each student lost his or her book.
PrepositionsShe is good in maths.She is good at maths.
Comparatives ("than")He is taller then me.He is taller than I (am).
Each / every (singular)Every boy and girl were present.Every boy and girl was present.
Misplaced modifierRunning fast, the bus was missed.Running fast, she missed the bus.
Pro tip tip

Find the verb, then strip the "of / with / along with" phrase before it. "The box of nails are" → cover "of nails" → "The box is". The phrase between subject and verb is a decoy.

03 The agreement traps that catch everyone

Two patterns supply a huge share of marks: a collective noun acting as one unit takes a singular verb, and "one of the X who/that…" uses a plural verb (it refers to the X's, not "one").

Reads fine, but wrong

"The committee are deciding." · "He is one of those players who scores often." Both verbs picked the wrong noun.

Correct

"The committee is deciding" (one unit). · "one of those players who score often" — who = the players, so plural.

Common trap gotcha

Collective nouns (team, committee, jury, family, government) acting as a single body take a singular verb — "the team is". And in "one of the X who/that…", the relative clause describes the plural X, so use a plural verb: "one of the books that are torn".

Pick the correct sentence.

"Each … has". Each is singular, so the verb is "has" — the "of the boys" plural is a decoy sitting between subject and verb.

04 Solve it fast

"He is taller ___ his brother." Fill the blank.warm-up
Solve it

Comparisons take than (not "then", which is about time). than.

The fast way shortcut

See "-er" or "more" → expect than. "Then" = next-in-time; never use it after a comparative.

Spot the error: "Each of the boys have a book."core
Solve it

The subject is each (singular), so the verb must be "has", not "have". have → has.

The fast way shortcut

Each / every / either / neither / one of are all singular. Mentally cover the "of the boys" phrase and the mismatch jumps out.

"The team ___ winning." (acting as one unit)stretch
Solve it

A collective noun treated as a single body takes a singular verb: the team is winning. is.

The fast way shortcut

Team / jury / committee / family as one unit → singular ("is"). Only go plural if the members act individually ("the team were arguing among themselves").
