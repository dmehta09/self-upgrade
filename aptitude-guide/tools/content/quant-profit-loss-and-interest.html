Profit, loss & interest.

Commerce sums are easy marks once you fix the base: profit % rides on cost, discount rides on marked price, and CI beats SI by the interest-on-interest.

01 The idea

Profit or loss % is always measured on the cost price. Selling price = cost × (1 ± profit%/100).

Every commerce question hinges on which number is the base. Name cost price (CP), marked price (MP) and selling price (SP) before touching a percentage.

In plain English analogy

Think of cost price as the start line of a race. Profit % measures how far past the line you finished — measured from the line, never from the finish. Change the start line and every percentage changes.

profit% = SP − CPCP × 100   # base is COST, not selling price · discount% is on MP

02 Marked price & discount

Shops mark up, then discount. Discount is a cut off the marked price (MP), not the cost. SP = MP × (1 − discount%/100), and profit is still judged against CP.

SP = MP × (1 − d/100)   # ₹250 marked, 20% off → 250 × 0.8 = 200
Common trap gotcha

Two bases, two traps: profit % is on cost (not on selling price), and discount % is on marked price (not on cost). Mixing them is the single biggest error here. Label CP / MP / SP first, then apply each percent to its own base.

03 Simple vs compound interest

Simple interest is the same flat amount each year. Compound interest earns interest on past interest, so it pulls ahead — first noticeably in year 2.

SI = P · R · T100     A = P(1 + R/100)n   # CI interest = A − P
Simple interest

₹1000 at 10% for 2 yrs: 100 + 100 = ₹200. Flat ₹100 every year, forever linear.

Compound interest

1000 × 1.12 = 1210 → interest ₹210. The extra ₹10 is interest on year-1's ₹100.

₹200SI, 2 yrs
₹210CI, 2 yrs
₹10the gap = P·(R/100)²
Pro tip tip

For 2 years the CI − SI gap is exactly P × (R/100)2. Here 1000 × 0.12 = ₹10 — skip computing both and read the gap straight off.

An article costs ₹500 and is sold for ₹600. The profit percent is:

20%. Base is the cost: 100 ÷ 500 = 20%. Dividing by the selling price (600 → 16.7%) is the classic wrong-base trap.

04 Solve it fast

A marked price of ₹250 carries a 20% discount. You pay?warm-up
Solve it

Discount is off the marked price: 250 × 0.8 = ₹200.

The fast way shortcut

20% off = pay ⅘. 250 × ⅘ = 200 in one step — never compute the discount, then subtract.

An item sold at ₹90 gives a 10% loss. Its cost price?core
Solve it

A 10% loss means SP = 0.9 × CP, so CP = 90 ÷ 0.9 = ₹100.

The fast way shortcut

Loss % is on cost, so divide by 0.9 (= × 10/9). Don't take 10% of 90 and add it — that uses the wrong base.

Compound interest on ₹1000 at 10% for 2 years?stretch
Solve it

A = 1000 × 1.12 = 1000 × 1.21 = 1210, so CI = 1210 − 1000 = ₹210.

The fast way shortcut

SI is 2 × ₹100 = ₹200; add the year-2 gap P·(R/100)² = ₹10 → ₹210. Reading CI as SI + the gap beats squaring 1.1.
