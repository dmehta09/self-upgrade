Averages & ages.

Average is just sum over count — but the speed comes from shifting the whole set at once, and from knowing that age gaps never change.

01 The idea

Average = sum ÷ count. So sum = average × count — and if you add k to every term, the mean rises by k.

Most "find the missing value" questions are really "recover the sum". Get the total, subtract what you know, done.

In plain English analogy

The average is the water level if you poured everyone's glass into one tank and shared it back flat. Add 5 to every glass and the flat level rises by exactly 5 — no need to re-pour.

average = sumn  ⇒  sum = average × n   # add k to each term → mean + k

02 Weighted average = alligation

When groups have different sizes, you can't average the averages directly — weight each by its count. That's exactly the alligation see-saw from mixtures.

weighted avg = n1·a1 + n2·a2n1 + n2   # the mean lands nearer the bigger group

Common trap gotcha

Don't average two speeds arithmetically. A trip out at 40 and back at 60 km/h is not 50 — equal distances need the harmonic mean (here 48). See the Time-speed-distance page for the 2xy/(x+y) shortcut.

03 Ages: the gap is frozen

Everyone ages at the same rate, so the difference between two ages never changes. Set the present unknown as x, write each person in terms of x, and shift by the same amount for past/future.

Father 40, son 10 — when is father twice the son?
The 30-year gap never moves. Twice as old means the gap equals the son's age, so son = 30 → x = 20 years on.
1

Name the present

Father = 40, son = 10 today.

2

Shift by x

In x years: 40 + x and 10 + x.

3

Set the condition

40 + x = 2(10 + x) → x = 20.

5 years ago the average age of a 4-person family was 24. The average today (same members)?

29. Adding 5 to every term lifts the mean by exactly 5 (not by 5 × count). 24 + 5 = 29.

04 Solve it fast

Average of 30 numbers is 20. Add 5 to each. New average?warm-up
Solve it

Adding a constant to every term adds it straight to the mean: 20 + 5 = 25.

The fast way shortcut

The count (30) is a distractor — a uniform shift moves the mean by the shift, full stop. Ignore n.

Average of 4 numbers is 15; three of them are 10, 20 and 12. The fourth?core
Solve it

Total = 15 × 4 = 60. Known three sum to 42, so the fourth = 60 − 42 = 18.

The fast way shortcut

Recover the sum first (avg × n), then subtract the knowns. Every "missing term" average is this one move.

Father is 40, son is 10. In how many years will the father be twice the son's age?stretch
Solve it

In x years: 40 + x = 2(10 + x) → 40 + x = 20 + 2x → x = 20.

The fast way shortcut

"Twice as old" means the fixed 30-year gap equals the son's age. So son must be 30 → that's 20 years from now. No equation needed.
