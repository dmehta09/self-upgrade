Direction sense.

Someone walks, turns, and walks again — where do they end up, and how far from the start? Draw it, and these become free marks.

01 The idea

Two rules solve almost everything: turning rotates your facing (right = clockwise, left = anti-clockwise), and final distance from start is the straight line — often a 3-4-5 triangle.

Never track directions in your head — draw the path. A quick sketch on the rough sheet turns a confusing word-problem into a picture you can read off.

In plain English analogy

It's a treasure map. "3 paces north, turn right, 4 paces" — you'd never solve that by imagining it; you'd draw arrows on paper and measure the shortcut back. Same here: the pen does the thinking.

02 Turning: right is clockwise

Face a direction, then turn. Right moves you clockwise (N→E→S→W); left moves anti-clockwise (N→W→S→E). Two turns the same way = a U-turn (180°).

The compass — right = clockwise
Facing North (accent arrow), a right turn swings you clockwise toward East.
Common trap gotcha

"Left" and "right" are relative to the walker's current facing, not the page. If they're walking South, their right hand points West — not East. Always re-orient from where they're looking now.

03 Distance from start = the straight line

When they ask "how far from the starting point", you want the shortest (straight-line) distance, not the total walked. Add up net North–South and net East–West, then use Pythagoras. Watch for the friendly 3-4-5 and 5-12-13 triples.

distance = √( (net N–S)2 + (net E–W)2 ) # 3 & 4 → 5
3 km North, then 4 km East
Net 3 North and 4 East → straight-line 5. Spot the 3-4-5 and skip the square root.

You walk 6 m East, 8 m North, then 6 m West. How far from the start?

8 m. +6 East and −6 East cancel to zero, so the net move is purely 8 m North. "Distance from start" ignores the path length.

04 Solve it fast

Facing East, you turn right, then right again. Which way are you facing?warm-up
Solve it

Right from East → South; right again → West. Two same-direction turns = 180°, so East → West.

The fast way shortcut

Two turns the same way = a U-turn. Just flip to the opposite direction; skip the step-by-step.

A man walks 5 km South, 12 km East. How far is he from start?core
Solve it

Net 5 South and 12 East → √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13 km.

The fast way shortcut

5 and 12 → recognise the 5-12-13 triple instantly. Memorise 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17.

At 6 a.m. facing the sun, you turn left. Which direction now?stretch
Solve it

At sunrise the sun is in the East, so you face East. Turn left (anti-clockwise) → North.

The fast way shortcut

Sunrise = East, sunset = West. From East, left is North (anti-clockwise). Shadows fall the opposite way — useful for shadow questions.
