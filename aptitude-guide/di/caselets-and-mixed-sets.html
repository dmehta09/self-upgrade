Data Interpretation · caselets

Caselets & mixed sets.

The same DI maths, but the data is buried in a paragraph instead of a tidy chart. Win these by doing one thing first: turn the prose into a small table.

01 The idea

A caselet is data hidden in sentences. Don't answer from the prose — extract every number into a tiny table, then read it like any other DI set.

Mixed sets link several charts/tables about one scenario, so a value from chart A feeds a question on chart B. Build the table once and every sub-question becomes a quick lookup.

In plain English analogy

It's like taking notes from a word problem in school. You'd never juggle "twice as many boys as girls, 90 total" in your head — you'd jot B = 2G, B + G = 90. A caselet is that, scaled up: write the grid, then solve.

02 Prose → table, every time

Read once with a pen. Each sentence drops one number into a row/column. The moment the table is full, the questions answer themselves.

1

Name the rows & columns

Spot the two dimensions — usually a category (cities, years, products) and a metric (count, revenue, %). Sketch the empty grid.

2

Drop each number in

Walk the prose sentence by sentence; place every figure in its cell. Mark unknowns with "?".

3

Fill blanks from totals

Use the row/column total to recover a missing value: missing = total − (the rest). This is the whole "missing-data" game.

4

Answer by lookup

Every sub-question is now a cell read or a one-step %/ratio. No re-reading the paragraph.

Worked extraction — "A class of 90 has twice as many boys as girls. 30 students play cricket; the rest play football":

GroupCountCricketFootball
Boys60??
Girls30??
Total903060

From "B = 2G and B + G = 90" → G = 30, B = 60. Football = 90 − 30 = 60. The grid did the work.

Common trap gotcha

Watch overlapping groups — "60 like tea, 50 like coffee, 20 like both" does not total 110. Picture a Venn: only-tea = 60 − 20, only-coffee = 50 − 20, so the union = 60 + 50 − 20 = 90. And compute a shared subtotal once, then reuse it across every sub-question — don't recompute the same total four times.

03 Mixed & missing-data sets

Mixed sets chain charts: a percentage from a pie applied to a total from a bar. Missing-data sets hide one cell and hand you the totals to recover it.

A team of 200 is 60% male; 30% of the whole team are managers. How many females are there?

80. 100% − 60% male = 40% female; 40% of 200 = 80. The "30% managers" is a deliberate distractor — it's about a different cut of the table, so ignore it for this question.

04 Solve it fast

A group of 200 is 60% male. How many females?warm-up
Solve it

Female share = 100% − 60% = 40%; 40% of 200 = 80.

The fast way shortcut

Go straight to the complement: 40% = 2/5, and 2/5 of 200 = 80. Don't find the 120 males first.

A firm reports revenue 500 and cost 350. What is the profit margin?core
Solve it

Profit = 500 − 350 = 150; margin = 150 ÷ 500 = 30%.

The fast way shortcut

Margin is on revenue, not cost: 150/500 = 15/50 = 30%. (Mark-up on cost would be 150/350 ≈ 43% — a classic mix-up.)

Sales rose from 400 (2018) to 500 (2019). What is the % growth?stretch
Solve it

Growth = (500 − 400) ÷ 400 = 100 ÷ 400 = 25%.

The fast way shortcut

Always divide the change by the earlier (base) year. 100/400 = ¼ = 25%. Dividing by 500 (the new value) is the trap — that would be 20%.
