Data Interpretation · charts & tables

Charts & tables.

A wall of numbers with five quick questions hanging off it. The marks aren't in the maths — they're in reading the right cell and approximating before you compute.

01 The idea

DI is reading + one quick calc, not heavy arithmetic. Lock onto the labels, units and scale first; then most answers are a single percentage, ratio or difference.

Tables give exact values; bars compare sizes; lines show a trend over time; pies split one whole into shares. Same skill every time — find the two numbers the question needs, ignore the rest.

In plain English analogy

It's a train timetable. You don't read the whole board — you find your row and your column, read one cell, and walk off. DI is the same: hunt the two numbers you need and ignore the rest of the grid.

02 Bars, lines & the pie formula

A bar chart compares quantities side by side — read each bar off the y-axis. A line chart is the same data joined up to show rise/fall over time. A pie splits 100% (= 360°) into slices.

Units sold (₹000s) — read the y-axis, don't eyeball
Four quarters: 40, 60, 80, 20. Total 200, average 50. Read the printed values — never judge "taller" by eye.
slice degrees = share% × 3.6  •  share% = degrees3.6 # 25% → 90° ; 90° → 25%
Common trap gotcha

Three traps in one: (1) always read the axis units & scale — a bar to "8" may mean 8 lakh, and a chart can start at 20, not 0. (2) Don't eyeball near-equal bars — read the printed value. (3) A pie slice is a % of the total, not an absolute count — convert with the total before comparing across two pies.

03 Fast reads: percentage, ratio & approximation

Speed comes from rough-first arithmetic and clean fractions. Round to friendly numbers, get the ballpark, then refine only if two options are close.

Slow way

"What % is 396 of 1980?" → long-divide 396 ÷ 1980 to four decimals, panic at the clock.

Fast way

Round: 400 ÷ 2000 = 1/5 = 20%. Pick the option nearest 20% and move on.

Percentage read
partwhole × 100. Spot the fraction: 45/180 = ¼ = 25%; 150/500 = 30%.
Ratio read
Cancel before dividing: 80 : 20 = 4 : 1; 60 : 90 = 2 : 3. Don't compute both totals.
% growth
increaseold × 100. 400 → 500 is 100/400 = 25%.
Approximate
Round to the nearest 10/100, compare, refine last. Ballpark kills 2–3 distractors instantly.

On a pie of total 8000, a department's slice is 90°. Its value?

2000. 90/360 = ¼; ¼ of 8000 = 2000. The degrees are a share, never the value — always convert through the total.

04 Solve it fast

On a pie chart, a category is 25% of spending. How many degrees is its slice?warm-up
Solve it

Degrees = share% × 3.6 = 25 × 3.6 = 90°.

The fast way shortcut

Anchor a few: 25% = 90°, 50% = 180°, 10% = 36°. Most slices are just sums of these.

Four bars read 40, 60, 80 and 20 (in thousands). What is the average?core
Solve it

Total = 40 + 60 + 80 + 20 = 200; average = 200 ÷ 4 = 50 (thousand).

The fast way shortcut

Pair to round numbers: (40+60) + (80+20) = 100 + 100 = 200, ÷ 4 = 50. Carry the "thousands" unit only into the final answer.

Two bars look almost the same height. What's the fastest reliable move?stretch
Solve it

Read the printed values (or the gridline each bar reaches). Eyeballing near-equal bars is exactly what the setter wants you to get wrong.

The fast way shortcut

If no values are printed, project each bar to the nearest gridline and compare those — never trust the pixels.
