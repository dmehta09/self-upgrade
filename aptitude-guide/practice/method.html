Practice · method

How to crack the test.

Aptitude rounds reward a plan, not heroics. Your score is attempts × accuracy under a hard clock — so the game is banking easy marks fast and never letting one stubborn question sink a whole section.

01 Score = attempts × accuracy

You don't need every question — you need the most marks per minute. Attempt the easy ones quickly and accurately; leave the time-sinks for a second pass.

In plain English analogy

It's a supermarket dash with a timer. You don't walk every aisle — you grab the cheap, easy wins first and only chase the high shelf if there's time left. One jar you can't reach isn't worth missing ten on the way.

Attempting 85% of a section at 90% accuracy scores higher than rushing all of it at 70% — and with negative marking the gap widens fast.

02 Spend time like a budget

Tests vary, but the shape is similar: roughly 40–60 seconds per question. Give each section a fixed slice and move on when it's spent — an unanswered easy question in the next section is worth more than a hard one you're still fighting.

1

Pass one — sweep

Answer everything you can in under ~45 s. Flag anything slower and skip it. This banks the bulk of the marks.

2

Pass two — flagged

Return to flagged questions with the time you saved. Now you can afford the longer ones.

3

Last minute — guess smart

If there's no negative marking, never leave a blank — eliminate and pick. If there is, only guess when you've cut it to a 50/50.

03 Protect your accuracy

Speed without accuracy just collects penalties. The fastest way to be accurate is to recognise the type and apply the shortcut — which is exactly what the topic pages drill into you.

Common trap gotcha

The sunk-cost trap: you've spent two minutes, so you spend two more "to not waste it". Wrong — those two minutes are gone either way. Flag it and move; protect the marks still on the table.

A section has negative marking (−¼ per wrong). You've eliminated two of four options on a question you can't fully solve. Best move?

Guess. With two left, P(right)=½: expected value = ½(+1) + ½(−¼) = +0.375 > 0. Eliminating first is what turns guessing from a gamble into a good bet.

04 Your prep playbook

How should I actually use this guide?warm-up
The plan

Read a topic page for the shortcut, do its 5-question mini-drill until you're quick, then come to the Timed drill and mix sections. Use the formula sheet and flashcards for daily recall.

The fast way shortcut

Learn → drill the topic → mix under time. Speed comes from the third step, so don't skip the clock.

I freeze on the section I'm weakest at. What now?core
The plan

Spend most of your prep on your weakest section — that's where marks are cheapest to gain. On test day, still bank your strong section first to build a cushion, then attack the weak one with a clear head.

The fast way shortcut

Prep weakest-first, attempt strongest-first. Beware sectional cut-offs — you usually must clear every section, so don't zero one out.
