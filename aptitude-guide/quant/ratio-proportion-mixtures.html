Ratio, proportion & mixtures.

Ratios share out a whole, proportions cross-multiply, and alligation cracks any "what blend gives this average" question on a single see-saw.

01 The idea

A ratio a : b is just parts. To split a total, add the parts and find one part; everything else is multiples of it.

Proportion means two ratios are equal — so you can cross-multiply. Mixtures ask which ratio two strengths combine in; alligation answers that without algebra.

In plain English analogy

A recipe says "2 cups flour to 3 cups sugar." That's 5 cups per batch. Want 20 cups? Each "part" is 4 cups → 8 flour, 12 sugar. Ratios are just the recipe; scaling is finding the cup size.

total = (a + b) parts  →  one part = totala + b   # share 60 as 2:3 → part 12 → 24 & 36

02 Chaining ratios & proportion

To join a : b and b : c, make the shared term match (scale to its LCM), then read a : c straight off. Proportion problems cross-multiply.

1

Spot the shared term

a:b = 2:3 and b:c = 4:5 share b (3 vs 4).

2

Scale to match

LCM(3,4)=12: a:b = 8:12, b:c = 12:15.

3

Read across

a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15 → a : c = 8 : 15.

ab = cd  ⇒  a × d = b × c   # cross-multiply to find the missing term
Common trap gotcha

Order matters. "A : B = 3 : 4" is not "B : A = 3 : 4". Pin each number to its name before you compute, and keep the same order across the whole chain — flipping one ratio silently inverts your answer.

03 Partnership: capital × time

When partners invest different amounts for different durations, profit splits in the ratio of capital × time — not capital alone.

sharei ∝ capitali × monthsi   # ₹6000·12 : ₹9000·8 = 72 : 72 = 1 : 1
72kA: 6000 × 12
72kB: 9000 × 8

04 Mixtures & alligation (the see-saw)

To hit a mean by blending a cheaper and a dearer item, the distances from the mean cross over. The ratio is (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper).

Alligation see-saw — blend ₹12 & ₹18 to ₹16
Cross the distances: the cheaper's share sits under the dearer gap. Ratio cheaper : dearer = 2 : 4 = 1 : 2.
Common trap gotcha

Alligation gives the ratio of the two parts — never the actual amounts. "Mix in 1 : 2" doesn't mean 1 litre and 2 litres; it means the smaller is half the larger. Use the total to convert the ratio into quantities.

Blend rice at ₹12/kg and ₹18/kg to get ₹16/kg. Ratio of cheaper : dearer?

1 : 2. 16 is closer to 18, so you need more of the dearer. The cheaper's share is the far distance (2), giving 2 : 4 = 1 : 2 — the cross is what trips people up.

05 Solve it fast

Divide 60 in the ratio 2 : 3.warm-up
Solve it

Parts = 2 + 3 = 5, so one part = 60 ÷ 5 = 12. Shares = 2×12 and 3×12 = 24 and 36.

The fast way shortcut

Always: total ÷ (sum of parts) = one part, then scale. No equations, no x.

If a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5, find a : c.core
Solve it

Make b common (LCM 12): a:b = 8:12, b:c = 12:15. So a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15 → a : c = 8 : 15.

The fast way shortcut

For a:c, just multiply the outer terms over the inner: (2×4) : (3×5) = 8 : 15. Matching b is the same thing, faster.

Mix 2 L of a 20% solution with 3 L of a 45% solution. Resulting strength?stretch
Solve it

Pure content = 2×0.20 + 3×0.45 = 0.4 + 1.35 = 1.75 in 5 L → 1.75 ÷ 5 = 35%.

The fast way shortcut

This is alligation in reverse: the mean splits 20 and 45 in the volume ratio 2 : 3, landing at 35 — closer to 45 because there's more of it.
