Time & work.

"A does it in 10 days, B in 15 — together?" The trap is to average days. The fix is to add rates, or better, run the LCM trick and work in clean whole units.

01 The idea

If someone finishes a job in n days, their rate is 1/n per day. Working together, you add the rates — never the days.

Days don't add; speeds do. Convert each worker to "fraction of the job per day", sum those fractions, then flip the total back to get the days the team needs.

In plain English analogy

Think of two taps filling a bucket. You don't average how long each takes alone — you add how much each pours per second. Two helpers each painting part of a wall daily finish faster precisely because their daily shares stack.

rate = 1n job/day  ·  combined = 1a + 1b  ·  days = 1combined rate

02 The LCM trick — kill the fractions

Adding fractions is slow. Instead, let total work = LCM of the days. Then each person does (total ÷ their days) whole units per day — clean integers all the way to the answer.

1

Set total work = LCM(days)

A in 10, B in 15 → total work = LCM(10, 15) = 30 units.

2

Find each rate in units/day

A = 30 ÷ 10 = 3/day; B = 30 ÷ 15 = 2/day.

3

Add the rates

Together = 3 + 2 = 5 units/day.

4

Divide total by combined rate

30 ÷ 5 = 6 days for the team. No fraction touched.

total = LCM(days)  ·  each rate = totalown days  ·  team days = totalΣ rates

A finishes in 12 days, B in 6 days. Using total work = LCM = 12, how many days together?

4 days. Total 12: A = 12÷12 = 1/day, B = 12÷6 = 2/day, together 3/day → 12 ÷ 3 = 4 days. Averaging or adding the days are both the classic wrong moves.

03 Pipes & cisterns + M·D/W

Pipes are time-and-work in disguise: an inlet adds to the rate, an outlet (leak) subtracts. And for staffing, workers × days ÷ work stays constant.

net rate = inlets − outlets # fill 1/4, empty 1/6 → 1/4 − 1/6 = 1/12 → 12 h
M1 · D1W1 = M2 · D2W2 # men · days per unit work is fixed
Common trap gotcha

Two slips to dodge: (1) add rates, not days — "10 days and 15 days" never gives 25 or 12.5; (2) an outlet pipe subtracts its rate. If the outlet is faster than the inlet the net rate is negative and the tank never fills.

04 Solve it fast

A does a job in 10 days, B in 15. What fraction do they finish in one day together?warm-up
Solve it

1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1⁄6 per day, so the whole job takes 6 days.

The fast way shortcut

LCM(10,15) = 30: A = 3/day, B = 2/day, sum 5/day → 5/30 = 1/6, and 30 ÷ 5 = 6 days. Whole numbers throughout.

12 workers finish a wall in 8 days. How long for 16 workers (same pace)?core
Solve it

More workers → fewer days (inverse). 12 × 8 = 16 × D → D = 96 ÷ 16 = 6 days.

The fast way shortcut

Keep the product M × D constant (same work): 12 × 8 = 96 man-days, ÷ 16 men = 6 days. No formula needed once you see it's just inverse proportion.

Pipe A fills a tank in 4 h; pipe B empties it in 6 h. With both open, how long to fill?stretch
Solve it

Net rate = 1/4 − 1/6 = 3/12 − 2/12 = 1/12 per hour → 12 hours.

The fast way shortcut

LCM(4,6) = 12 units: inlet +3/h, outlet −2/h, net +1/h → 12 ÷ 1 = 12 h. The outlet subtracts — that's the whole question.
