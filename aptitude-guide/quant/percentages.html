Percentages.

The single most-tested quant idea — and the engine behind profit/loss, interest, and data interpretation. Master the fraction conversions and you'll answer most of these in your head.

01 The idea

A percent is just a fraction with denominator 100. "x% of y" = (x/100) × y — and crucially, x% of y = y% of x.

Percentages let you compare unlike things on one scale. The exam reward isn't the definition — it's converting the ugly ones to clean fractions so you never long-multiply.

In plain English analogy

"Percent" is "per 100" — slice everything into a pizza of 100 pieces. 20% just means 20 of those 100 slices, so 20% of a thing is the thing split into fifths (20/100 = 1/5). The whole game is spotting which simple fraction a percent really is.

x% of y = x100 × y  =  y% of x # 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = 4

Pros don't compute 37.5% of 64 — they see 3/8 × 64 = 24. These conversions turn arithmetic into one division. Learn them cold.

Pro tip tip

Flip awkward problems with x% of y = y% of x. "What is 8% of 50?" is scary; "50% of 8 = 4" is instant.

03 Successive change = multiply, don't add

The number-one percentage trap: a 20% rise then a 20% fall is not a wash. Stack changes by multiplying their factors — a +20% is ×1.2, a −20% is ×0.8.

final = start × (1 ± a/100) × (1 ± b/100) # 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 → 4% net FALL
The trap (adding)

+20% then −20% → "0% net". Wrong — the second 20% is taken off a bigger number.

The fix (multiplying)

1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → a 4% net fall. Any "up a%, down a%" always loses a²/100 %.

Common trap gotcha

"Increase then decrease by the same %" is a favourite distractor — the tempting answer is always 0%. It's a net loss of (a²/100)%. For 10% that's 1%; for 20% it's 4%.

A salary is cut 10%, then the new salary is raised 10%. Net effect?

Down 1%. 0.9 × 1.1 = 0.99 — a 1% net fall. Same-size up/down always loses (a²/100)%, here 100/100 = 1%.

04 Solve it fast

What is 37.5% of 64?warm-up
Solve it

37.5% = 3/8. So 3/8 × 64 = 3 × 8 = 24.

The fast way shortcut

See the fraction, not the decimal: 64 ÷ 8 = 8, ×3 = 24. No long multiplication.

A number increased by 25% becomes 75. The original number?core
Solve it

1.25 × n = 75 → n = 75 / 1.25 = 75 × 4/5 = 60.

The fast way shortcut

+25% means ×5/4, so reverse with ×4/5. 75 × 4/5 = 60. Don't set up an equation.

In an election of 8000 votes, the winner got 60%. By how many votes did they win?stretch
Solve it

Two candidates → loser got 40%. Margin = 60% − 40% = 20% of 8000 = 1600.

The fast way shortcut

Don't find both totals — the margin is just the % gap: 20% of 8000 = 1600.
