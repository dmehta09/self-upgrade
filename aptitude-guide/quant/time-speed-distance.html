Time, speed & distance.

One triangle — speed, distance, time — powers trains, boats, and races. Nail the unit switch and "add or subtract speeds" rule, and these turn into 30-second wins.

01 The idea

Everything hangs off one relation: speed = distance ÷ time. Cover the one you want with your thumb and the other two show the formula.

The exam rarely asks the raw formula. It hides it inside trains, rivers, and two-person chases — so the real skill is the unit switch and knowing when speeds add vs subtract.

In plain English analogy

It's a money triangle. Distance is the cash, speed is your hourly rate, time is hours worked: cash = rate × hours. Know any two and the third is one division — no setup, no algebra.

speed = distancetime  ·  distance = speed × time  ·  time = distancespeed

02 The unit switch: ×5/18 and ×18/5

Trains are timed in seconds but quoted in km/h — so you must convert. Multiply km/h by 5/18 to get m/s, and m/s by 18/5 to go back. Memorise it like a reflex.

km/h → m/s : × 5/18   |   m/s → km/h : × 18/5 # 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s
72 km/h= 20 m/s
36 km/h= 10 m/s
54 km/h= 15 m/s
Pro tip tip

The two "5"s tell you the direction: going to the smaller unit (m/s) the 5 is on top (×5/18); going back up to km/h the 18 is on top.

03 Relative speed & trains crossing

Two movers? Use one frame. Opposite directions add the speeds; same direction subtracts them. A train crossing an object travels its own length + the object's length.

Opposite = add · Same = subtract
Head-on: 60 + 40 = 100 km/h closing speed. Chasing: 60 − 40 = 20 km/h — the gap shrinks slowly.
relative speed = a + b # opposite   or   a − b # same way
cross a pole: distance = train length  ·  cross a platform: = train + platform length
Common trap gotcha

A train crossing a platform (or bridge) must clear it fully, so it covers its own length + the platform length, not just the platform. A pole has no length, so there you use the train length alone.

A 150 m train at 20 m/s crosses a 250 m platform. Time taken?

20 s. The train clears the platform only after its tail leaves the far end, so distance = 150 + 250 = 400 m → 400 ÷ 20 = 20 s. Forgetting the train's own length is the classic miss.

04 Boats & streams + average speed

A river just adds or removes the boat's speed. Downstream = boat + stream, upstream = boat − stream. For a there-and-back trip at two speeds, average speed is harmonic: 2xy ÷ (x+y).

downstream = b + s  ·  upstream = b − s # b = boat in still water, s = stream
avg speed (equal distances) = 2xyx + y # go at x, return at y
Common trap gotcha

For equal distances, average speed is not the plain mean (x+y)/2 — you spend more time at the slower speed, so the true average is the harmonic mean 2xy/(x+y), which is always lower. Mean is right only for equal times.

05 Solve it fast

Convert 90 km/h to m/s.warm-up
Solve it

× 5/18: 90 × 5 ÷ 18 = 450 ÷ 18 = 25 m/s.

The fast way shortcut

90 ÷ 18 = 5 first, then × 5 = 25. Cancel before you multiply — never compute 450 ÷ 18 the long way.

A boat does 10 km/h in still water; the stream is 2 km/h. Find downstream and upstream speeds.core
Solve it

Downstream = 10 + 2 = 12 km/h; upstream = 10 − 2 = 8 km/h.

The fast way shortcut

Stream helps you go (add) and fights you coming back (subtract). Half their sum gives the boat speed, half their difference gives the stream — handy when the question gives you the two trip speeds instead.

A car goes to a town at 40 km/h and returns at 60 km/h. Average speed for the round trip?stretch
Solve it

Equal distances → harmonic mean: 2 × 40 × 60 ÷ (40 + 60) = 4800 ÷ 100 = 48 km/h.

The fast way shortcut

Don't pick 50 (the plain mean) — that's the trap. Use 2xy/(x+y) and the answer is always a touch lower: 48, not 50.
