Alerting with Alertmanager.

Alert rules live in Prometheus — they evaluate a PromQL expression on a schedule. Routing, grouping, deduplication, and silencing live in Alertmanager. Keeping these two responsibilities separate is what makes the system composable and survivable.

01 Alert rules in Prometheus

A rule defines a PromQL expression that, when true for a sustained duration (for:), transitions to FIRING and Prometheus sends the alert to Alertmanager.

The for: duration prevents flapping — a transient 1-second spike won't page anyone if the rule says "must be true for 5 minutes." Labels on the rule become labels on the alert, used for routing.

alerts.yaml
groups:
  - name: api_alerts
    rules:
      - alert: HighErrorRate
        expr: |
          sum by (service) (
            rate(http_requests_total{status=~"5.."}[5m])
          )
          /
          sum by (service) (
            rate(http_requests_total[5m])
          ) > 0.05
        for: 5m
        labels:
          severity: page
          team: backend
        annotations:
          summary: "High error rate on {{ $labels.service }}"
          description: "Error rate is {{ $value | humanizePercentage }}"
In plain English analogy

Prometheus is the smoke detector — it notices something is wrong. Alertmanager is the smart switchboard that decides: bundle all the "smoke in building" alarms into one call, figure out who's on duty right now, and send it to the right pager — not a separate call for every detector on every floor.

02 Alertmanager: routing & grouping

Alertmanager receives raw alerts from Prometheus and applies four operations before dispatching: grouping, deduplication, inhibition, and silencing. The routing tree then sends the group to the right receiver.

Grouping is the key step. Without it, a single deployment failure spawns 200 individual alerts — one per pod.
Grouping
Combine alerts that share the same group_by labels into one notification. E.g. all service=checkout alerts → one PagerDuty incident.
Deduplication
If the same alert keeps firing, send one notification — not one per evaluation cycle.
Inhibition
If a critical "datacenter down" alert fires, suppress all the child "service down" alerts that are obviously caused by it.
Silences
Mute matching alerts for a time window (e.g. during scheduled maintenance) without touching rules.
Receivers
PagerDuty, OpsGenie, Slack, email, webhook. One routing tree, multiple receivers based on severity and team labels.

03 Avoiding alert fatigue

Alert fatigue kills on-call. The root causes are almost always the same: too granular (per-instance instead of per-service), too many (alerting on symptoms and all their possible causes), and no for: duration.

Alert fatigue anti-pattern
  • Alert per pod: 200 pods = 200 alerts for one deployment failure
  • Alert on CPU > 80% — who cares if requests are healthy?
  • No for: — fires on every transient spike
  • All alerts page on-call, even INFO-level
Actionable alerting
  • Group by service: one alert per service, regardless of pod count
  • Alert on error rate or latency SLO — user-visible symptoms
  • for: 5m — must be sustained to page
  • Severity tiers: page only for SLO-breach; ticket for the rest
Alert on symptoms, not causes gotcha

Alerting on "CPU > 80%" or "disk > 90%" is alerting on causes. Users don't care about CPU — they care if requests are slow or failing. Alert on the SLO (error rate, latency) and let the dashboard guide root-cause analysis. The SLO module covers symptom-based alerting in depth.

A deployment rolls bad to all 50 pods simultaneously. Without Alertmanager grouping, how many pages does on-call receive?

Right. Without grouping configuration, Alertmanager dispatches every individual alert. With group_by: [service], all 50 collapse into one incident notification.

04 Interview rapid-fire

What's the difference between an alert rule in Prometheus and routing in Alertmanager?core
Model answer

Alert rules live in Prometheus — they define when something is wrong (a PromQL condition held for a duration). Alertmanager decides what to do with those alerts: group related ones, deduplicate, inhibit child alerts, silence during maintenance, and route to the right receiver (PagerDuty, Slack). Separation of concerns: detection vs dispatch.

Say it out loud say it

"Rules say 'this is broken'; Alertmanager says 'group it, silence it if maintenance, then route it to the right team.'"

How do you reduce alert fatigue in a large microservices system?core
Model answer

Three levers: (1) group by service, not instance; (2) alert on user-visible symptoms (error rate, latency) not internal causes (CPU, memory); (3) use a sustained for: duration to ignore transient spikes. Tier your alerts — only SLO breaches page on-call; everything else creates a ticket.

Say it out loud say it

"Group by service, alert on symptoms not causes, sustain with for: duration, and only page for SLO breaches."

Follow-ups to expect
  • What is inhibition and when would you use it?
  • How does SLO-based alerting work? (tee-up to next module)
What is inhibition in Alertmanager?warm
Model answer

Inhibition suppresses lower-priority alerts when a higher-priority source alert is already firing. Example: if "datacenter unreachable" fires, suppress all the "service X down" alerts that are obviously caused by the same outage. It prevents the on-call from being flooded with symptoms when the root cause is already alerted.

Say it out loud say it

"If the parent cause is already alerting, inhibit the child symptoms. Datacenter down → suppress all the individual service-down alerts it causes."
