The 2026 stack: open source + vendors.

The OSS observability stack has standardized around OTel for instrumentation and a Prometheus/Loki/Tempo/Grafana core for storage and visualization. Understanding how each piece fits — and where commercial platforms play — is table-stakes for any senior engineering conversation.

01 The OSS core

Think of it as a pipeline: OTel instruments, the Collector routes, specialized backends store, Grafana visualizes, Alertmanager pages you.

Each component is replaceable. That's the power: you can swap Tempo for Jaeger v2, or ship directly to Datadog, without changing your application instrumentation — because OTel owns that layer.

In plain English analogy

OSS stack = building your own PC. You pick the CPU (Prometheus), the storage (Mimir/Thanos), the display (Grafana). High control, assembly required. Datadog / New Relic = a prebuilt laptop. Plug it in, pay the bill. Less assembly, less control, vendor lock-in.

OpenTelemetry (OTel)
Vendor-neutral standard. Traces + Metrics APIs stable; Logs maturing; Profiles public alpha (Mar 2026). One wire protocol: OTLP (gRPC/HTTP).
Prometheus 3.x
Pull/scrape model. Native histograms (experimental), native OTLP ingestion, Remote-Write 2.0. Scale out via Mimir or Thanos.
Grafana Alloy
The OTel-Collector distro from Grafana — ships logs to Loki, metrics to Prometheus/Mimir, traces to Tempo.
Loki
Log backend: small label index + compressed chunks. Cheap. Query with LogQL. Correlates to traces via trace_id.
Tempo / Jaeger v2
Trace backends. Tempo = object-storage-only + TraceQL. Jaeger v2 rebuilt on OTel Collector; v1 hit EOL Dec 2025.
Pyroscope
Continuous profiling backend for OTel Profiles (4th signal).
Grafana 12.x
Git Sync (dashboards as code), Drilldown GA, dynamic dashboards, managed alerts/recording rules.
Alertmanager
Routes Prometheus alerts → PagerDuty/Slack/email. Handles grouping, inhibition, silences.

02 Commercial platforms

Every major vendor now supports OTLP ingest. The decision is really about operations burden vs. control vs. cost at scale.

Vendor Model Strength Choose when…
Datadog All-in-one SaaS; host + APM pricing Polished UX, deep APM, built-in anomaly detection Team wants zero infra ops and can afford per-host cost at scale
New Relic All-in-one SaaS; usage-based pricing Broad coverage, predictable pricing at small scale Smaller team wanting a single pane without per-host sticker shock
Honeycomb Event-based SaaS; "observability 2.0" High-cardinality wide events, BubbleUp exploration Team debugging complex microservice interactions with rich per-request context
Splunk On-prem or cloud; log/SIEM heritage Compliance, security correlation, log search at scale Enterprise needing logs + security in one platform; legacy Splunk investment
Grafana Cloud Managed Prometheus/Loki/Tempo/Pyroscope OSS-compatible, generous free tier, lower ops burden Team already using OSS stack and wanting managed hosting without relearning tools
OSS DIY Self-hosted Prometheus + Loki + Tempo + Grafana Full control, no vendor lock-in, lowest marginal cost Platform team with Kubernetes expertise and willingness to own the ops burden

03 2026 trends to know

OTel decouples instrumentation from backend
You instrument once with OTel; route OTLP to any backend. Switching vendors no longer means re-instrumenting code.
Wide structured events gaining ground
One rich event per request (Honeycomb model) rather than separate logs/metrics/traces. OTel spans are effectively this format.
Profiles going mainstream
OTel Profiles (4th signal, public alpha Mar 2026) + Pyroscope bring continuous profiling into the same pipeline as traces and metrics.
Common trap gotcha

Vendor lock-in via proprietary agents. If you instrument with the Datadog agent or New Relic agent directly (bypassing OTel), migrating later requires touching every service. Instrument with OTel first, export to whatever backend. Switching becomes a config change in the Collector, not a code change.

Your team re-instruments with OTel and wants to send traces to both Tempo (OSS) and Honeycomb for evaluation. Where do you make that routing change?

Right. The OTel Collector is the routing layer. Add an exporter for each destination and wire it into the pipeline — no application code changes. This is exactly why the Collector is the recommended deployment pattern.

04 Interview rapid-fire

Walk me through the 2026 OSS observability stack.core
Model answer

OTel SDK instruments services and emits OTLP. The OTel Collector (or Grafana Alloy) receives, processes, and routes to backends: Prometheus for metrics, Loki for logs, Tempo or Jaeger v2 for traces, Pyroscope for profiles. Grafana 12 visualizes all signals, Alertmanager handles routing. OTel is the key: instrument once, swap backends via config.

Say it out loud say it

"OTel instruments, the Collector routes, then Prometheus/Loki/Tempo/Grafana store and visualize. The big idea is that OTel decouples instrumentation from backend — I can switch vendors by changing Collector config, not app code."

When would you choose Datadog over the OSS stack?core
Model answer

Datadog makes sense when the team wants zero ops burden on the observability infrastructure, needs polished APM and anomaly detection out of the box, and the per-host cost is acceptable at their scale. The OSS stack wins when you have a platform team willing to run it, need full data control, or the Datadog bill becomes prohibitive at large scale.

Say it out loud say it

"Datadog when you want plug-and-play and can afford the per-host pricing. OSS when you have a platform team and want cost control. The good news is OTel lets you start with either and migrate later."

What does OTel actually solve? Why does it matter in 2026?stretch
Model answer

OTel breaks vendor lock-in at the instrumentation layer. Before OTel, switching from Datadog to Grafana Cloud meant re-instrumenting every service. Now you instrument once with OTel (Traces + Metrics stable, Logs maturing, Profiles in alpha), emit OTLP, and route it anywhere via the Collector. The ecosystem converged on this: even Datadog and New Relic accept OTLP ingest.

Say it out loud say it

"OTel is the USB standard for telemetry. Before it, you were locked into one vendor's agent. Now you instrument once and the Collector routes OTLP to any backend. Even proprietary vendors accept it."
