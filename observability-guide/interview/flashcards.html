  • What is cardinality in the context of metrics?
    tap to flip
    The total number of unique time series a metric generates — the product of all unique label-value combinations. High cardinality (e.g. adding user_id as a label) can OOM Prometheus.
  • What is a trace exemplar?
    tap to flip
    A data point attached to a histogram bucket that carries a trace_id from a real request. In Grafana, clicking an exemplar jumps directly to the correlated trace in Tempo — no manual searching.
  • What is OTLP?
    tap to flip
    OpenTelemetry Protocol — the vendor-neutral wire format for all three signals (logs, metrics, traces) over gRPC or HTTP/protobuf. Replaces Jaeger Thrift, Zipkin JSON, etc. Prometheus 3.x ingests OTLP natively.
  • Head sampling vs tail sampling — what's the key trade-off?
    tap to flip
    Head sampling decides at trace start — cheap but blind to outcomes, misses rare errors and slow traces. Tail sampling buffers the full trace then decides — keeps the interesting ones but costs Collector memory.
  • What is an error budget?
    tap to flip
    The allowed amount of unreliability in an SLO. For 99.9% over 30 days: 0.1% × 43 200 minutes = 43.2 minutes of allowed downtime. Budget healthy → ship fast. Budget gone → freeze releases and invest in reliability.
  • What is a burn rate and when does 14.4× matter?
    tap to flip
    Burn rate = how fast you're spending error budget vs. sustainable pace. 1× = on budget. 14.4× on the 1-hour window means you'd exhaust the monthly budget in ~50 hours — the standard page threshold for multi-window SLO alerts.
  • RED method — what does it stand for?
    tap to flip
    Rate (requests/second), Errors (error rate), Duration (latency, typically p95/p99). A service-level dashboard pattern for request-driven services. Pairs with USE for infrastructure resources.
  • USE method — what does it stand for?
    tap to flip
    Utilization (% busy), Saturation (queue depth / backlog), Errors. Applied to resources (CPU, disk, network) rather than services. Tells you when a resource is the bottleneck before it causes user impact.
  • What is p99 (99th percentile)?
    tap to flip
    The value below which 99% of observations fall. For latency: at most 1% of requests are slower than p99. Averages hide tail latency — p99 exposes what slow users experience. Always alert on percentiles, not mean.
  • What is a native histogram (Prometheus 3.x)?
    tap to flip
    A histogram with automatic exponential bucket boundaries — no need to pre-define buckets. Better resolution at high values, lower cardinality. Still experimental in Prometheus 3.x; enabled via --enable-feature=native-histograms.
  • What are OTel semantic conventions?
    tap to flip
    Standardized attribute names for common operations — http.request.method, db.system, messaging.system. HTTP + database + messaging are stable (~v1.41). They make instrumentation portable across teams and dashboards reusable.
  • What does Prometheus "scrape" mean?
    tap to flip
    Prometheus pulls (scrapes) metrics by HTTP GET to a service's /metrics endpoint on a configured interval. The pull model gives simple auth and no retry storms — but short-lived jobs may be dead at scrape time (use Pushgateway).
  • What is LogQL?
    tap to flip
    Loki's query language. Select log streams by label, then pipe through filters and parsers. Example: {service="checkout"} |= "ERROR" | json | duration > 1s. Inspired by PromQL but operates on log lines, not time series.
  • What is TraceQL?
    tap to flip
    Grafana Tempo's query language for searching spans. Find spans by attribute: { span.http.status_code = 500 && duration > 1s }. Lets you search across millions of traces without knowing the trace_id upfront.
  • SLI vs SLO vs SLA — one line each.
    tap to flip
    SLI = what you measure (e.g. success ratio). SLO = internal target (e.g. 99.9%). SLA = customer contract (e.g. 99.5%) — looser than SLO so breaching SLO doesn't immediately trigger contract penalties.
  • What is the critical path in a distributed trace?
    tap to flip
    The longest sequential chain of spans — their combined duration equals the total end-to-end latency. Parallel spans don't add to the total. Only spans on the critical path matter for latency optimization.
  • What is context propagation?
    tap to flip
    The mechanism that carries trace_id and span_id across service boundaries — typically in HTTP headers (W3C traceparent). Without it, each service creates an isolated trace and you can't stitch a distributed call into one end-to-end view.
  • What does the OTel Collector do?
    tap to flip
    A standalone pipeline: receive OTLP → process (filter, enrich, tail-sample) → export to one or more backends. Decouples apps from backend choices. Changing backends = Collector config change, not a code deploy.
