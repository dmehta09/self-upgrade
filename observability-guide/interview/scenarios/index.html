Interview prep · scenarios

Debugging scenarios.

Incident walkthroughs are where interviews are won or lost. These two scenarios teach the investigation method and give you a story to narrate, step by step.

01 The investigation method

Drive from metrics → traces → logs. Form a hypothesis, confirm with a signal, narrow — repeat until you have a root cause.

In plain English analogy

Think of it like diagnosing a car. The dashboard warning light (metric alert) tells you something is wrong. Plugging in a diagnostic reader (trace) tells you it's the fuel injector on cylinder 3 — not the whole engine. The technician's notes (logs) say the last mechanic used the wrong grade of fuel. You wouldn't start by reading the notes — you'd start at the dashboard.

1

Alert → confirm user impact

Is the SLO burn rate elevated? Check the RED dashboard: which endpoint, which version, what error rate? Confirm user-facing impact before going deeper.

2

Trace → localize the span

Click a trace exemplar from the histogram. Open the waterfall. Find the span with the worst duration or error. Is it on the critical path? Which service owns it?

3

Hypothesis → check with a signal

Form a hypothesis: "The DB query is slow because of a missing index." Verify it: jump from the span to correlated Loki logs (filtered by trace_id). Look for the smoking gun.

4

Fix → confirm SLO recovers

Apply the fix (hotfix, rollback, config change). Watch the error budget burn rate return to 1× or below. The budget graph is your "did it work?" signal.

Common trap gotcha

Jumping straight to logs is the most common interview mistake. Without a trace to narrow the needle, you're searching millions of log lines blindly. Always localize with a trace first, then use logs to understand the why.

02 The scenarios

Each scenario starts with an alert and walks the full investigation. Practice narrating them out loud — that's the interview format.

p99 latency spike

SLO burn alert fires. Walk the trace waterfall to find a slow DB span on the critical path.

Error-rate surge

Fast burn-rate alert. Correlate 5xx errors to a bad deploy. Rollback; watch budget recover.
