Fundamentals · consistency

Consistency, CAP & PACELC.

When data lives on many machines, "what's the current value?" stops being obvious — different replicas can briefly disagree. Consistency is the promise about how fresh a read is. CAP and its 2026-standard refinement PACELC tell you the unavoidable trade-offs. This is the concept interviewers probe hardest, so we'll make it concrete.

01  Strong vs eventual consistency

Two ends of a spectrum:

🎯

Strong consistency

Every read returns the most recent write, always. Simple to reason about, but requires coordination between replicas — which costs latency and availability.

🌊

Eventual consistency

Replicas may briefly disagree, but converge "eventually" (usually milliseconds). Fast and highly available; you must tolerate reading slightly stale data.

Useful middle grounds: read-your-own-writes (you always see your own latest change), monotonic reads (you never see time go backwards), and causal consistency (effects never appear before their causes).

In plain English analogy

Two bank tellers sharing one ledger. Strong: they lock the book so only one writes at a time — never wrong, sometimes you wait. Eventual: they each scribble in their own copy and reconcile every few seconds — never blocked, but for a moment your balance might look off. A bank picks strong; a "likes" counter happily picks eventual.

02  The CAP theorem

CAP says: when a network partition happens (some machines can't talk to others — and at scale, it will), you must choose between two of three:

C — Consistency
Every read sees the latest write (or an error).
A — Availability
Every request gets a non-error response (possibly stale).
P — Partition tolerance
The system keeps working despite dropped messages between nodes.

Since partitions are a fact of distributed life, P is non-negotiable — so the real choice during a partition is C vs A:

  • CP (consistency over availability): refuse requests on the minority side rather than risk stale data. Banking, inventory, locks.
  • AP (availability over consistency): keep answering everywhere, reconcile later. Feeds, likes, shopping carts, presence.

03  PACELC — the part interviewers want in 2026

CAP only describes behaviour during a partition — which is rare. PACELC adds the everyday case: "if Partition, then A or C; Else (normal operation), Latency or Consistency." Even with a healthy network, keeping replicas in lockstep costs latency. So the deeper trade-off is constant: how much latency will you pay for fresher reads?

How to use this in an interview

Decide per-data, not per-system. In one design you might keep payments strongly consistent and the activity feed eventually consistent. Saying "I'd use strong consistency here because a double-charge is unacceptable, but eventual for the like-count because a 2-second lag is invisible" is exactly the judgement they're listening for.

During a network partition, a shopping-cart service keeps accepting "add to cart" on both sides and merges later. Which choice is that?

Right: staying available on both sides and reconciling later is the AP choice — a great fit for carts, where losing a "stay available" moment costs sales and the rare merge conflict is easy to resolve.
