Expert · billing & subscriptions

Design billing, subscriptions & metering.

The layer that decides how much to charge and when — plans, subscriptions, and (increasingly) usage-based metering — sitting above the payment system that moves the money. It's a correctness problem, not a throughput one: metering is high-volume (billions of events/day), but the bar is never double-charge, never leak revenue, always reproducible and auditable. Idempotency and an append-only ledger are the spine.

01  Requirements & scale

✅

Functional

Plans/prices; subscriptions (seats); usage metering (ingest → aggregate → rate); invoicing with proration + tax; dunning on failed payments.

📐

Non-functional

Idempotent everywhere (a double-counted event = overcharge); reproducible/auditable rating; the billing ledger is the source of truth.

02  API & data model

POST/v1/usage{ event_name, customer_id, value, ts, identifier } → 202 (async; dedup on identifier)
POST/v1/subscriptions · PUT /v1/subscriptions/{id}create; change plan with proration_behavior
GET/v1/invoices/{id}lines[], subtotal, tax, total, amount_due
Price book
products → prices → tiers, immutable + versioned — you never mutate a price a customer is on; you create a new version. Read-mostly, heavily cached.
Metering
usage_events(event_id PK, meter, customer, value, ts) append-only; usage_aggregates(customer, meter, period, agg_value) rolled up.
Subscription state machine
trialing → active → past_due → (canceled | unpaid), plus incomplete (first invoice unpaid) — these are literally Stripe's statuses.

03  Architecture: ingest → aggregate → rate → invoice

Metering is async/eventually-consistent (events trickle in, late events happen); invoicing is a transactional point-in-time snapshot that freezes usage at cycle close. Getting that freeze boundary right is the crux.

04  Usage metering: ingest → aggregate → rate

Each stage has a correctness trap. Ingest must be idempotent — at-least-once delivery is a given, so every event carries a client-generated identifier and the store enforces uniqueness (double-counting overcharges the customer — the cardinal sin). The meter is append-only; corrections are new compensating events, never edits.

meter.sql
-- 1) Idempotent ingest: ON CONFLICT makes a duplicate event_id a no-op (no double count).
INSERT INTO usage_events (event_id, meter, customer_id, value, ts)
VALUES ($1, $2, $3, $4, $5)
ON CONFLICT (event_id) DO NOTHING;           -- dedup is the whole game

-- 2) Pre-aggregate per meter/customer/period into compact buckets so invoicing
--    reads rollups, not raw events. (sum | count | max | unique-count per meter.)
SELECT customer_id, date_trunc('hour', ts) AS bucket,
       sum(value) AS agg_value, max(ts) AS last_event_ts
FROM   usage_events
WHERE  meter = $1 AND ts >= $period_start AND ts < $period_end
GROUP  BY customer_id, bucket;

Late / out-of-order events are the hard part. A query-over-immutable-events model (Orb-style) handles them naturally — a late event just changes the query result. Define a watermark / grace window: finalize the invoice in draft at cycle end, hold a few hours for stragglers, then finalize; truly-late events become adjustments on the next invoice. (Stripe rejects events older than 35 days.)

05  Pricing models

The volume-vs-graduated distinction is a classic interview test: same 6 units, same tiers, but $39 (volume) vs $41.50 (graduated). Keep rating pure and reproducible — same aggregate + same price version → the same amount forever (needed for audit/recompute).

06  Invoicing, proration & dunning

Invoice run
At each cycle boundary: recurring items + frozen rated usage + prorations + credits → subtotal → tax (Stripe Tax / Avalara, computed at finalize by geo) → total → draft → finalize → charge. Draft/finalize separation lets late usage land before the invoice is immutable; reduce a finalized invoice with a credit note, never an edit.
Proration
Mid-cycle plan change: credit unused time on the old price + charge the prorated new. $10→$20 halfway = −$5 + $10 = +$5. Behaviors: settle on next invoice / invoice immediately / none. Downgrades usually become credit balance, not a cash refund.
Dunning
On a failed renewal: active → past_due, smart retries (Stripe: up to 8 over ~2 weeks; sweet spot 4–6), decline-code aware (soft insufficient_funds → delay + retry; hard decline → request new card, don't retry), grace period, then canceled/unpaid. Retries reuse the same idempotency key. Failed payments are a large share of involuntary churn.

07  Correctness, observability & 2026

Ledger & RevRec
The double-entry ledger is the source of truth — invoices/charges/refunds/credits post balanced entries; balances are derived. Billing ≠ revenue: recognize revenue when the obligation is satisfied (ASC 606) — usage as consumed, annual upfront ratably — via a RevRec layer.
Failure modes
Double-counting (dedup + idempotent aggregation), missed events (reconcile raw vs rated; alert on per-tenant rate anomalies), late events (watermark), revenue leakage (an "unbilled usage" dashboard), double-charge (idempotency end-to-end).
Metrics
Ingest p99 + dedup rate, aggregation lag, invoice success rate, failed-payment recovery rate (target ~60–75%), reconciliation breaks → 0, MRR/ARR + net revenue retention.
2026 landscape
Stripe Billing + meters (sum/count/last; acquired Metronome ~$1B, Jan 2026 — "metered pricing is the native model for the AI era"); Orb (SQL billable metrics over raw events); Lago (open-source). Usage-based / hybrid pricing is the structural shift driving event-based metering.

08  Trade-offs & interview recap

Your metering pipeline receives usage events at-least-once (clients retry). Why is the event's identifier the most important field?

Right: at-least-once delivery means duplicates are inevitable, and a double-counted usage event is a double-charge — the cardinal billing sin. The unique identifier + a uniqueness constraint makes ingestion idempotent; the meter is append-only and corrections are compensating events.
Say this to nail it

"Billing sits above payments and is a correctness problem. The metering pipeline ingests usage idempotently (dedup by event id — double-count = overcharge), stores append-only, pre-aggregates per period, and rates against an immutable, versioned price book — keeping rating pure so it's reproducible for audit. Invoicing freezes the aggregate at cycle close with a grace window for late events, applies proration (volume vs graduated math) and tax, and posts to a double-entry ledger. Failed payments go through smart-retry dunning. The whole thing is idempotent end-to-end because a double-charged customer is a disaster."
