Fundamentals · multi-region

Multi-region, DR & data residency.

One availability zone is a single blast radius. Multi-region is how you survive a metro outage, cut latency for global users, and satisfy residency laws — but every cross-region write pays in lag, conflict risk, or cost. Interviewers want RPO/RTO numbers and a clear active-active vs active-passive choice, not just "we'd deploy in three regions."

01  Active-active vs active-passive

🔁

Active-passive (hot standby)

One region serves traffic; the other replicates (often async) and takes over on failover. Simpler conflict story; RTO includes DNS/health-check failover time; standby capacity is paid for and mostly idle.

⚡

Active-active

All regions serve traffic. Needs GeoDNS/latency routing, data locality or conflict resolution, and careful sticky/home-region writes for strong data. Higher utilization; harder correctness.

02  RPO & RTO — say the numbers

RPO — Recovery Point Objective
How much data can you lose? Measured as time: async replication with 30 s lag ⇒ RPO ≈ 30 s. Sync/semi-sync cross-region can approach RPO ≈ 0 at the cost of write latency.
RTO — Recovery Time Objective
How long until you're serving again? Includes detection, failover, DNS propagation, cache warm, and "are we sure the old primary is fenced?"

Interview move

State RPO/RTO as product requirements first ("payments: RPO 0, RTO < 1 min; feed: RPO 5 s OK"). Then pick the topology. Don't invent five-nines multi-region without a budget.

03  GeoDNS, latency routing & stickiness

Users need to land in the right place:

  • GeoDNS / latency-based DNS (Route 53, Cloudflare) — map client geography to the nearest healthy region.
  • Anycast — same VIP announced from many PoPs; BGP sends packets to the closest. Great for edge/gateways; careful with TCP mid-session moves.
  • Health-checked failover — remove an unhealthy region from the pool; pair with connection draining.
  • Stickiness / home region — for RYOW and strong writes, pin a user or tenant to a home region and only fail over on hard outage.

Chat gateways, URL shorteners at the edge, and ride-hailing city shards all lean on "nearest healthy" — see session guarantees when the nearest replica is stale for the author.

04  Data residency & conflict resolution

Data residency
Keep PII / regulated rows in the user's legal region; replicate only non-PII metadata or encrypted blobs if needed. Multi-tenant SaaS often pins a tenant to a region at signup.
Last-write-wins (LWW)
Timestamp (or hybrid logical clock) wins. Simple; can lose concurrent updates. Fine for presence, profile "last status."
CRDTs / merge
Commutative merges (counters, sets, maps) converge without coordination. Great for collaborative state; heavier to reason about.
Home-region authority
One region owns an entity; others forward writes. Avoids conflicts at the cost of cross-region hop for that entity.
Split-brain on failover

If the "dead" primary comes back writing while the new primary is live, you fork history. Fence the old primary (STONITH, lease expiry, generation numbers) before promoting. Name fencing when you describe automated failover.

05  DR runbook — what you'd say on the whiteboard

1

Detect

Regional health: synthetic probes from outside the region, error-budget burn, replication lag alerts — not just "host down."

2

Decide

Is it AZ-local (shift load inside region) or region-wide? Who has authority to declare failover?

3

Fence & promote

Stop writes to the old primary; promote replica; verify generation / watermark.

4

Redirect traffic

DNS/anycast/weight flip; drain connections; watch error rates and lag.

5

Reconcile & learn

Replay outbox / catch-up replication; reconcile money systems; postmortem and game-day the runbook.

Product needs "no lost payments" across a region outage, and can tolerate ~80 ms extra write latency. What's the honest design?

Right: RPO ≈ 0 for money means you pay latency (or a carefully designed dual-write + reconciliation). LWW and multi-second async lag are the wrong conflict/RPO tools for a ledger.
