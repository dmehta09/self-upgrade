Practice · interview simulation

Mock-interview drills.

Reading case studies builds knowledge; talking through one under a clock builds the skill. Each drill below is a real interview question run on the standard 35-minute loop — phased timer, interviewer-style nudges, and a rubric written from what senior interviewers actually listen for. Stand up, talk out loud, draw on paper. Then grade yourself honestly.

01  How a 35-minute loop runs

The timer mirrors the phase budget from the design playbook. Each phase auto-advances when its budget runs out — exactly the pressure you'll feel in the real thing — but you can move on early and bank the time.

PhaseBudgetYou should be…
Requirements5 minAsking clarifying questions; locking scope and non-functional targets.
Estimation5 minDeriving QPS, storage, bandwidth out loud — rounding hard.
High-level design10 minDrawing the boxes and walking one read and one write through them.
Deep dive10 minGoing deep on the 1–2 hardest components and their trade-offs.
Wrap-up5 minFailure modes, bottlenecks, monitoring, what you'd build first.
Talk to the empty room

The single highest-leverage habit: say everything out loud, as if the interviewer were there. Silent thinking reads as silence in a real loop. If you can narrate a design to nobody, narrating it to a person becomes easy.

03  How to self-grade honestly

✅

Tick it only if you said it

Thinking it doesn't count; the interviewer can only grade what they heard. If you realised "I should have asked about celebrities" during the deep dive, that rubric line stays unticked.

↻

Rerun cold, days later

A drill you aced an hour after reading the lesson proves recall, not skill. Rerun it in a few days without rereading — the score that survives the gap is your real score.

Scores persist in your browser (per question, best and history), the same local-only storage the lesson progress uses. A reasonable bar before a real senior loop: 80%+ on every core drill, cold.
