First, the one diagram behind every design

Almost every system is the same handful of boxes between a user and their data — a load balancer, some stateless servers, a cache, a database. Press ▶ and watch a single request travel through them. This animated player reappears on every case study, drawn for that specific system.

If you remember one thing

Scaling a system is mostly about answering, for each of these boxes: how do I add more of them, and what breaks when I do? Every concept in the Foundations track is a tool for one of those boxes; every case study wires them together for a real product.

Two tracks: the concepts, then the designs

Learn the building blocks once in Foundations, then see them combined in the Case studies. New to system design? Go top to bottom. Brushing up before an interview? Jump straight to a design and follow the links back to any concept you're shaky on.

1

Get the fundamentals

Estimation, the latency numbers, availability, and the consistency models (CAP/PACELC). The vocabulary every later page assumes.

→ Fundamentals

→ Building blocks · Data at scale

Warm up on small designs

URL shortener, rate limiter, unique-ID generator — small enough to hold in your head, but they teach the framework.

→ Warm-up designs
4

Work the core & advanced cases

News feed, chat, Dropbox, Uber, YouTube, payments, LLM serving — each adds a signature concept on top of the basics.

→ Core & Advanced designs
5

Drill the interview itself

Knowledge isn't the skill. Run timed 35-minute mock-interview drills — phased clock, interviewer nudges, and a senior grading rubric — until 80%+ feels routine.
