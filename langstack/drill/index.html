3 modes · timed
Practice · quiz drills

Reading about it isn't knowing it.

Three timed drills test the judgment the lessons build: which tool does this job, which primitive solves this requirement, and what broke given only the symptom. Wrong answers come with the why — and a link back to the lesson.

00  How a round works

the clock
Each question has its own countdown (30–45 s). Run out of time and it counts as wrong — speed IS the skill being trained.
the chips
Click an answer or press 1–9. Every question explains itself afterwards; the near-miss answers carry a "the trap" note.
the score
Your best per mode is saved in this browser (localStorage). Questions reshuffle every round, so replaying is the point.
Be honest with yourself

If you guessed and got lucky, count it as a miss in your head and read the linked lesson anyway. The drills only work if a green chip means "I could justify this to a teammate."

01  Which tool? 30 s / question

The first judgment call of every project: is this a plain SDK call, a LangChain chain, a LangGraph graph, or a LangSmith problem? Four chips, fixed positions, no excuses.

02  Design it 45 s / question

You've chosen the stack — now pick the primitive: reducer or checkpointer? Supervisor or handoff? interrupt or conditional edge? These are the pairs that get confused in real designs.

03  Debug it 45 s / question

Every lesson's gotcha callouts, weaponized: you get the symptom, you name the cause. This is the drill that pays off at 2 a.m.
