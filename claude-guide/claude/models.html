Using Claude · lesson 2

The model family

The current family is the newest flagship, Fable 5, plus the 4.x trio — Opus, Sonnet, and Haiku, named like a progression from grandest to shortest. They share the same skills; they trade capability against speed and cost. Picking the right one for the job is most of “using Claude well.”

01  The flagship + three sizes

Opus

The most capable of the 4.x line. Deep reasoning, long-horizon agentic coding, the hardest problems. Slower and priciest — bring it out when the task is genuinely hard.

Sonnet

The balanced default. Excellent intelligence at much better speed and cost. The workhorse for most coding and app work.

Haiku

The fastest and cheapest, with near-frontier quality. Ideal for high-volume, latency-sensitive, or simpler tasks.

The newest: Fable 5 note

Fable 5 (claude-fable-5) sits above the 4.x trio as the newest, most capable model — Anthropic's frontier for reasoning and agentic work. When you're building AI applications and want the best Claude has, default to it; the trio below is how you trade down for speed and cost.

Pick the right vehicle analogy

You don't take a moving truck to buy milk, or a scooter to move house. Haiku is the scooter — quick and cheap for small errands. Sonnet is the everyday car. Opus is the truck — heavier and costlier, but it hauls the loads nothing else can.

In Claude Code you switch with /model (alias opus/sonnet/haiku or a full ID); in the API you pass the ID. Exact versions roll forward — the tiers stay stable.

03  Context windows & thinking

The context window is how much text a model can consider at once (the same idea as context in Claude Code). A 1M-token window can hold an entire large codebase or a stack of documents in one go.

Most current models can also do extended thinking — spend extra steps reasoning before answering, which sharply improves math, logic, and multi-step problems. You spend a little more time and tokens for a more reliable answer. In Claude Code, the /effort slider tunes how hard the model works.

A simple default tip

Start with Sonnet. Move up to Opus — or all the way to Fable 5 — when a task is genuinely hard or stalls; drop to Haiku when you need speed/volume and the task is simple. You rarely go wrong starting in the middle.

You're building a high-traffic feature that classifies support tickets — simple, but millions per day. Which model fits best?
Right. Match the model to the job. Simple + high-volume + latency-sensitive is Haiku's sweet spot; paying for Opus there would burn money and time for no real gain.
