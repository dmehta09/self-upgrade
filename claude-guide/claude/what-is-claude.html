Using Claude · lesson 1

What is Claude (and an LLM)?

Claude is Anthropic's family of large language models (LLMs) — AI systems trained to read text and produce a helpful, fluent response. It's the “brain” you met in Track B: the thing Claude Code calls to decide what to do. Here we look at the brain itself.

01  What an LLM actually does

Under the hood, an LLM does one deceptively simple thing: given some text, it predicts what comes next, one chunk (a “token”) at a time. Do that prediction extremely well, having read a huge slice of human writing and code, and something remarkable emerges — it can answer questions, write, summarize, translate, and reason through problems.

An improv partner who's read everything analogy

Think of an actor doing improv who has read nearly every book, manual, and conversation. You give them a line; they continue the scene convincingly. They're not looking facts up in a database — they're drawing on patterns they absorbed. That's why LLMs are brilliant and occasionally make things up: continuing plausibly isn't the same as being certain.

This guide stays Claude-focused. For how LLMs are trained, tokens, embeddings and the rest, see your sibling GenAI guide. Here we care about using Claude well.

02  What makes Claude, Claude

Claude is built by Anthropic with a strong emphasis on being helpful, honest, and harmless. In practice you'll notice it's good at careful reasoning, following nuanced instructions, long-context work, and writing & reviewing code — which is exactly why it powers Claude Code.

Reads more than text

Modern Claude models are multimodal: they take images and PDFs as input, not just words.

Can think before answering

On hard problems Claude can do extra reasoning (“extended thinking”) to work through steps — more in Power features.

Can use tools

Given tool definitions, Claude can decide to call them — the foundation of agents like Claude Code. See Tool use.

Comes in sizes

Opus, Sonnet, Haiku — a range from most-capable to fastest-and-cheapest. That's the next lesson.

03  Two honest limits

It can be confidently wrong gotcha

Because it predicts plausible text, Claude can state something false as if it's sure (a “hallucination”). For anything that matters, give it sources, ask it to show its work, and verify — the same instinct as closing the loop in Claude Code.

It also has a knowledge cutoff — training data only goes up to a certain date — so for recent events or your private data, you give it that context (in the prompt, via tools, or via files).

At its core, what is an LLM like Claude doing?
Right. It's next-token prediction at scale. That's why it's so capable and why it can hallucinate — and why giving it good context and a way to verify matters so much.
