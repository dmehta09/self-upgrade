Using Claude · lesson 4

Prompting 1 · be clear & direct

A prompt is just your instructions to Claude — and the quality of the answer tracks the quality of the ask. The number-one rule isn't a trick: be clear and direct. Treat Claude like a sharp new colleague on their first day, who has zero context about your situation until you give it.

01  Three things a good prompt has

Context

Who's it for, what's the situation, what background does Claude need? Don't make it guess.

A clear task

One specific thing to do, in plain language. “Summarize,” “rewrite,” “find the bug in,” “draft.”

The shape of “done”

Format and constraints: how long, what structure, what to include or avoid.

Briefing an intern, not a mind-reader analogy

A brilliant intern still can't read your mind. “Make this better” gets a shrug; “Tighten this paragraph to two sentences, keep the friendly tone, cut the jargon” gets exactly what you wanted. Same person — the difference is the brief.

02  Feel the difference

Flip between the two prompts below and press Ask Claude. Same task, two levels of clarity — watch the reply (and the “why”) change. You can edit the prompt and ask again.

03  Quick wins

Show the format
Ask for bullets, a table, JSON, or “just the code.” If output shape matters, say it.
State constraints
Length, tone, audience, what to avoid. “Under 100 words, no marketing fluff.”
Give the material
Paste the text, the error, the data. Claude can't see what you don't include.
Prefer positive instructions
“Write in plain English” beats “don't be too technical” — tell it what to do, not just what to avoid.
This is the same skill as Claude Code tip

“Add a /health endpoint that returns {status: ok} and a test” worked in Track B for exactly this reason: a clear task with a clear definition of done. Prompting well in chat is prompting well in Claude Code.

Which prompt will reliably give you something usable on the first try?
Right. Format (2 sentences), channel/audience (Twitter), tone (upbeat), and a must-include (free trial). Specific in, specific out.
