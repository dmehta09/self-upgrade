Reference

Timed APOSD drills.

Reading about red flags is one thing — naming them in five seconds, mid code-review, is the actual skill. Three timed chip-quiz modes turn the book's judgment calls into reflexes. Misses link straight back to the lesson that fixes them.

01 How a round works

The clock
45 seconds per question. The bar drains; silence counts as a wrong answer — just like the pause before you answer in a review.
The chips
Click an answer or press 1–9. In the first two modes the chips never move, so you get faster every round. In Fix it, a tempting near-miss always sits beside the right move.
The score
Every answer is explained — the trap chips most of all. Your best score per mode persists in this browser (localStorage["aposd-drill"]). Questions reshuffle each round.
Keyboard tip

1–9 answer · Enter/→ next question · Esc back to the intro. A round takes about five minutes.

02 Spot the flag

A symptom from real code → which red flag is it? Shallow vs overexposed, leakage vs temporal — the confusions this guide exists to settle.

03 Pick the principle

A design scenario → which principle decides it? Deep vs hide vs pull-down sound alike until a clock is running.

04 Fix it — choose the move

You've spotted the smell — now pick the right move, with the plausible-but-wrong one sitting right next to it. The chips change per question in this mode.

05 Keep sharpening

For slower-paced revision, flip the flashcards or keep the red-flags cheatsheet within reach. For the full arguments behind every chip, the red-flags playbook ties the whole book together — and the spotter there lets you find the flags in real code, not just name them.
