Judgment

Design it twice & software trends.

Hard-won judgment, part one. The cheapest way to get a better design is to invent two; and the popular trends — OOP, agile, TDD, patterns, and now AI-assisted coding — are tools to be judged by one question: does it reduce complexity here?

01 Design it twice

Before committing, sketch at least two genuinely different designs and compare them. Even experts rarely nail the best structure on the first try.

In plain English analogy

It's trying on two outfits before leaving the house. You learn more from the contrast in thirty seconds than from staring at one option for an hour. The act of comparing is what reveals what “good” even means here.

1

Sketch 2+ approaches

Make them different, not variations — different module boundaries, not different variable names.

2

List the trade-offs

Which interface is simpler? Which hides more? Which copes better with the likely change?

3

Pick — or combine

Often the winner is a hybrid: the best idea from each. The comparison is the value; it's an hour that saves weeks.

03 AI-assisted coding, judged the same way

AI changes who types the code, not what design is for. Ask the same question of it as of every trend: does it reduce complexity here?

In plain English analogy

A coding assistant is a brilliant new teammate with no memory of your codebase — every session, it joins fresh. What does a new teammate need to be productive and safe? Small, honest interfaces; hidden internals; obvious code. Exactly what this book has been teaching all along.

  • Deep modules matter more, not less. An assistant works interface-first inside a bounded context window — it can't hold your whole system in its head any better than a human can. Deep modules with small interfaces are precisely what fits; information hiding limits how much it must read before a change is safe.
  • The tactical tornado now works at machine speed. “It works” has never been cheaper to produce, so the strategic-investment decision is worth more. Review is the new bottleneck — and obvious code is the cheapest code to review.
  • Comments and names became shared context. They steer human readers and code generators alike. Write the comments first and the contract doubles as the prompt.

04 What about performance?

Performance deserves more than a paragraph — it gets its own lesson: measure first, design around the critical path, decide what matters. Continue to Performance & deciding what matters.

Red flag gotcha

Premature optimisation, cargo-culted patterns, and reflexive getters/setters all add complexity for benefits you haven't proven you need. Reach for a trend because it makes this system simpler — never because it's popular.

05 Check yourself

You have a working first design. What does “design it twice” recommend?

Compare a different second design. The point is contrast between real alternatives (different boundaries), not cosmetic variants. An hour of comparison routinely beats the first idea — even for experts.
